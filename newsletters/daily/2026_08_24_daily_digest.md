<h2 style="margin: 0 0 8px; font-size: 24px; font-weight: 800; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.2;">Tesla (TSLA) +5.14% to $362.86 after Nevada clears up to 5,000 paid robotaxis across Clark County</h2>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">Nevada opens Clark County to up to 8,000 paid robotaxis; Tesla gets the biggest single-operator ceiling at 5,000 and rallies 5% on Friday.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
Nevada's Transportation Authority unanimously approved full Autonomous Vehicle Network Company permits for Tesla, Waymo, and Uber, replacing an interim order that had capped Tesla at just 10 robotaxis on a narrow slice of the Las Vegas Strip. Together the three permits authorize up to 8,000 driverless vehicles for paid rides across Clark County over 12 months.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
Tesla is authorized for up to 5,000 robotaxis; Waymo and Uber (branded "Aviari") each receive up to 1,000. Operators must begin commercial service within 120 days and file vehicle inspections, insurance certificates, and fare tariffs with the NTA before collecting fares.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
This is the first US state to authorize five-figure paid robotaxi fleets. TSLA closed Friday at $362.86 (+5.14%) on 58.5M shares (~1.39x average volume), removing the acute regulatory overhang and giving the AV-Ride-Hail bull case a concrete deployment path ahead of Q3 earnings.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>
Tesla and AV thematic traders, autonomous-vehicle regulatory watchers.
</p>

<h2 style="margin: 0 0 8px; font-size: 24px; font-weight: 800; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.2;">US-Canada talks collapse — 50% Section 338 tariffs on ~$20B of Canadian goods live at midnight; Carney vows dollar-for-dollar retaliation</h2>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">First-ever Section 338 tariffs — 50% duties on ~$20B of Canadian goods live after Friday-night talks collapse.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
US-Canada negotiations collapsed before a midnight Friday deadline, sending Section 338 tariffs live at 12:01 a.m. ET Saturday, Aug 22. The duties add a 50% surcharge on roughly $20 billion of Canadian imports — cement, softwood lumber, hockey sticks, building materials, and certain apparel.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
This is the first presidential invocation of Section 338 of the Tariff Act of 1930 — a statute unused since it became law — which lets the White House hit any trading partner that "discriminates" against US commerce with duties up to 50%. Prime Minister Carney suspended talks and vowed matching tariffs dollar for dollar.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
After SCOTUS struck down IEEPA-based duties in February, Section 338 opens a new legal channel for tariff action. Canadian retaliation heading into Jackson Hole raises near-term risks for autos (Big Three US-Canada supply chains), homebuilders (softwood lumber), and USD/CAD.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>
Macro traders, autos and homebuilder desks, USD/CAD traders.
</p>

<h2 style="margin: 0 0 8px; font-size: 24px; font-weight: 800; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.2;">Deere (DE) Q3 FY26 — EPS $5.10 vs $4.45 est, revenue $12.6B (+5%), FY26 guide raised; CEO calls 2026 "bottom of the ag cycle"; stock +6.94% to $620.94</h2>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">Deere blows past Q3 FY26 estimates, raises full-year net-income guide, and calls 2026 the bottom of the ag equipment cycle.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
Deere Q3 FY26 net income came in at $1.379B ($5.10 per diluted share vs $4.45 est) on revenue of $12.608B (+5% Y/Y) vs a $10.92B estimate. Management raised full-year net-income guidance to $4.75B–$5.0B and shares closed +6.94% at $620.94.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
Construction &amp; Forestry led with +18% revenue and +84% operating profit. Small Ag &amp; Turf rose +12% at 18.4% operating margins. Precision Ag dipped 6%. Direct FY26 tariff expense is ~$1.1B gross, ~$750M net after refunds.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
CEO John May explicitly called 2026 "the bottom of the current ag equipment cycle," citing improving orders and used-equipment health. Construction's dominance and rising forward expectations are read-throughs for AGCO, CNH, and Caterpillar's construction segments.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>
Industrials PMs, ag-cycle traders, tariff watchers.
</p>
