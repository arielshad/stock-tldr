<h2 style="margin: 0 0 8px; font-size: 24px; font-weight: 800; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.2;">Brent tops $100 for first time since May — Houthi strikes on Saudi tankers in the Red Sea</h2>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">Houthi strikes on Saudi tankers push Brent above $100 for the first time since May.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
Yemen's Houthi movement struck two Saudi oil tankers — Encelia and Layla — in the Red Sea with missiles and drones on July 23, citing a declared blockade of Saudi ports. The Saudi Press Agency confirmed a fire at the bow of the Encelia; all crew were reported safe.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
Brent crude rallied 6.45% to close at $101.10 — its first close above $100 since May 26 — while WTI climbed 5.37% to $91.49. Five Saudi oil tankers reversed course in the Red Sea after the Houthi blockade declaration.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
The Red Sea corridor and the Strait of Hormuz together carry the bulk of Saudi crude to global buyers — both are now under active pressure simultaneously. Higher oil at $100 tightens the Fed's cutting path heading into next Wednesday's July 29 FOMC decision.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>
Energy desks, macro traders, tanker shipping allocators, and anyone watching the July 29 FOMC.
</p>

<h2 style="margin: 0 0 8px; font-size: 24px; font-weight: 800; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.2;">Trump imposes Section 301 forced-labor tariffs on 60 trading partners — effective Friday July 24</h2>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">New Section 301 duties on 60 trading partners replace the expiring 10% global baseline — effective 12:01 a.m. Friday.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
The Trump administration announced tariffs of 10% to 12.5% on 60 US trading partners it accuses of failing to crack down on forced labor in supply chains. Duties take effect 12:01 a.m. ET Friday July 24, replacing the flat 10% global baseline tariff that expired the same morning.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
Most covered countries — including China and Vietnam — face a 12.5% rate. The UK, Canada and Mexico get 10%. Exemptions include oil and gas, sector-specific tariffs (steel, autos), and most USMCA-compliant goods. Coverage spans roughly 99% of US imports.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
The step-up to 12.5% for most partners hits import prices right as Brent closes above $100 — a two-way CPI squeeze heading into the July 29 FOMC. Affected partners from Canberra to Brasília signaled they'd keep negotiating rather than retaliate, at least for now.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>
Trade desks, macro strategists, importers, and apparel and consumer discretionary analysts.
</p>

<h2 style="margin: 0 0 8px; font-size: 24px; font-weight: 800; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.2;">Apple (AAPL) +3.53% — Apple Maps to power Ford's 2027 Universal EV Platform via new MapKit for Automotive SDK</h2>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">Ford becomes the launch partner for Apple's new MapKit for Automotive SDK, embedding Apple Maps natively into its 2027 Universal EV Platform independent of iPhone or CarPlay.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>

Ford is the first automaker in the world to deploy the SDK.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
MapKit for Automotive runs Apple Maps directly on vehicle displays — turn-by-turn directions, real-time traffic, intelligent EV routing, and battery preconditioning — independent of any connected iPhone. Ford's Latitude AI feeds road-level data from Apple Maps into next-gen BlueCruise (Level 2 in 2027, Level 3 targeted 2028). Morgan Stanley raised AAPL PT to $364 (from $360).
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
It's Apple's first meaningful auto-OS win since shuttering Project Titan in 2024 and turns Apple Maps into a factory-fit automotive layer competing directly with Google Automotive Services — the platform GM chose when it phased out CarPlay.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>
Auto-tech analysts, AAPL longs, autonomy watchers; next catalysts are Apple Q3 FY26 earnings and Ford Q2 earnings on July 30.
</p>
