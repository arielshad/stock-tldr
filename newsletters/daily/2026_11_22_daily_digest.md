<h2 style="margin: 0 0 8px; font-size: 24px; font-weight: 800; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.2;">Bitcoin crashes to two-year low near $55,130 — bounces 9%, $1.8B in crypto liquidations</h2>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">Bitcoin prints a two-year low at $55,130 as Friday's AI selloff spills into the crypto complex; Strategy drops below its BTC cost basis.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
After the Nasdaq fell 5.3% on Friday Nov 20, selling continued into Saturday when Bitcoin pierced $60,000 and reached an early-Saturday low of $55,130 — its weakest print since September 2024. By early Saturday afternoon ET it had rebounded ~9% to around $60,300.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
CoinGlass tallied ~$1.8B of leveraged crypto positions wiped out in 24 hours (~$1.3B in longs). US spot Bitcoin ETFs had ~$1.1B of net outflows for the week; BlackRock's IBIT alone saw $541.1M of outflows on Thursday — the largest single-day outflow since its January 2024 launch.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
CryptoQuant called it "extreme capitulation" — holders realized ~$11.3B of losses over seven days, roughly 8× the gains. Strategy (MSTR) finished the week down 30.3% at $77.80 and now trades with Bitcoin below the firm's ~$75,452 average cost per coin.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>
Crypto desks, MSTR holders, and macro traders watching Monday's US cash open as the next key test.
</p>

<h2 style="margin: 0 0 8px; font-size: 24px; font-weight: 800; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.2;">Intuit beats Q1 FY27, sells Mailchimp to Bending Spoons for $3.68B — but cuts FY27 guide, shares -12.8%</h2>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">Intuit beats Q1, agrees to sell Mailchimp to Bending Spoons for $3.68B, but cuts FY27 revenue guide on Credit Karma weakness.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
Intuit reported fiscal Q1 2027 (quarter ended Oct 31) after the close on Wednesday Nov 19 and announced it will sell Mailchimp to Italy's Bending Spoons for about $3.68B. Headline numbers topped estimates but management trimmed full-year revenue guidance on a weaker Credit Karma outlook, sending the stock down ~12.8% on Friday.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
Revenue was $4.354B (+12% Y/Y), non-GAAP EPS $2.91 (+56%). The Mailchimp sale is $3.31B cash + $368M in Bending Spoons stock, closing early 2027 — with proceeds earmarked for additional share repurchases. Updated FY27 revenue guide: $23.081–$23.365B (+8–9%); Credit Karma guide dropped to $2.721–$2.826B (+3–7%).
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
Intuit paid ~$12B for Mailchimp in 2021 and is now selling it for $3.68B — a significant write-down. The Credit Karma guide cut cites higher rates and faster shift of financial search toward AI, a pressure that also hangs over NerdWallet and lead-gen fintech broadly.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>
Fintech and software investors; M&amp;A watchers tracking the valuation reset in SaaS acquisitions from the 2021 era.
</p>
