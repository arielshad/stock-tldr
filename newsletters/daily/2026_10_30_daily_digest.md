<h2 style="margin: 0 0 8px; font-size: 24px; font-weight: 800; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.2;">Alphabet (GOOGL) -12.64% — worst day ever, ~$500B in market cap gone</h2>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">Alphabet's -12.64% day erases ~$500B of market cap — biggest in company history, 2nd-biggest ever on a US stock.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
On Thursday October 29 Alphabet closed down 12.64% at $283.82, shedding roughly $482–$500B of market value — the biggest single-day loss in the company's history and the second-biggest ever on a US-listed stock behind NVIDIA's January 2025 drawdown.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
The sell signal was spending, not demand — Q3 capex of $51.4B and the CFO flagging a "significant" further 2027 step-up produced negative FCF of -$9.07B against ~$2.7B expected, despite +25% revenue growth and Google Cloud +103% to $30.7B.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
AI-capex bulls now need a return-on-capital story, not just revenue growth. Alphabet's +25% revenue and +103% Cloud were not enough to offset -$9.07B of FCF. The S&P 500 slipped 0.3% and Nasdaq fell 1.4%, snapping a 3-day winning streak, while Amazon and Intel then followed with sharp after-hours drops on their own FCF and demand concerns.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>
Mega-cap growth investors, options-flow desks, and breadth watchers tracking index concentration risk.
</p>

<h2 style="margin: 0 0 8px; font-size: 24px; font-weight: 800; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.2;">Amazon (AMZN) Q3 2026 — AWS +39% to $46B, record 13.9% op margin; stock -7–10% AH on $220B capex</h2>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">AWS re-accelerates to +39% and backlog hits $578B, but a $220B capex plan drops TTM FCF to -$42.4B and AMZN sinks 7–10% AH.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
Amazon reported Q3 2026 Thursday October 29 after the close. Net sales grew 12% to $201.9B — in line with the company's $197–$202B guide — while operating income of $28.1B (+61%) hit a record 13.9% consolidated margin, above the $22.5–$26.5B guide.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
AWS net sales rose 39% to $46.0B — fastest growth in nearly five years — with a $578B backlog and 38.7% segment margin. The company guided Q4 revenue of $240–$247B, reiterated 2026 capex of ~$220B, and management said AWS growth will exceed 40% in both 2027 and 2028. TTM FCF swung to -$42.4B after a $94.8B Y/Y increase in property-and-equipment spend.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
AWS re-accelerating from +32% to +39% confirms hyperscaler AI demand is still outrunning supply. But the TTM FCF flip to -$42.4B echoes the same free-cash-flow narrative that punished Alphabet — capex suppliers (NVDA, AVGO, ANET) see demand confirmed even as Amazon's own cash generation absorbs the investment.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>
Mega-cap growth investors, AI infrastructure bulls, and cloud-share analysts tracking AWS vs. Azure vs. Google Cloud.
</p>

<h2 style="margin: 0 0 8px; font-size: 24px; font-weight: 800; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.2;">FOMC holds at 3.75–4.00% on a 10–2 split — Cook and Hammack dissent for a hike</h2>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">Fed holds at 3.75–4.00% in a 10–2 split as Cook and Hammack dissent for a quarter-point hike.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
The FOMC voted on Wednesday October 28 to leave the federal funds target range unchanged at 3.75–4.00%, following the 25bp increase at the September 16 meeting. The vote was 10–2 — Governor Lisa D. Cook and Cleveland Fed President Beth M. Hammack both dissented in favor of a quarter-point rate hike.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
The statement describes activity "expanding at a strong pace" with strong productivity, solid business investment, and employment gains matching labor force growth, while "inflation remains high." Chair Warsh again provided no explicit rate path, stating the Committee will gather more information to determine the appropriate policy stance.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
The 10–2 split is the first multi-member hawkish dissent of the Warsh chairmanship, signaling that part of the Committee views the September hike as insufficient against sticky inflation. The hold matched pre-meeting OIS pricing (~90% probability) but the dissent pattern and absence of forward guidance keeps December live for either another hike or an extended hold.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>
Rates traders, macro desks, and bank-funding analysts tracking Fed policy under Chair Warsh.
</p>
