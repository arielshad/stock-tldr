<h2 style="margin: 0 0 8px; font-size: 24px; font-weight: 800; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.2;">Brazil's Ibovespa -13.88% — worst day since March 2020 after Lula wins runoff</h2>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">Lula's surprise runoff win over Flávio Bolsonaro triggered Brazil's biggest one-day equity crash since March 2020 — Ibovespa -13.88%, BRL -8.4%, EWZ -19.8%.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva won a fourth non-consecutive term in Brazil's Oct 25 presidential runoff with roughly 51.79% of valid votes. Markets had positioned for a Flávio Bolsonaro win on the back of pre-election polling that showed him narrowly ahead.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
Monday the Ibovespa fell 13.88% to 176,608 from Friday's 205,082 close; B3's circuit breaker activated after a 10% drop — the first halt since March 2020. USD/BRL closed at R$5.4154 (+8.4%), EWZ ETF closed down 19.8% at $34.15. Domestic banks were hardest hit: Bradesco -21.70%, Itaú -13.66%, while dollar-earner Vale rose +4.01%.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
Investors had read a Bolsonaro-led administration as more likely to push fiscal reforms. The combined index + FX move left Ibovespa roughly -20% in USD terms. The next key signal is Copom's Nov 4 Selic decision, while a hostile Congress sets up legislative gridlock.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>
EM macro, Latam equity, and BRL FX traders tracking the fallout from the election result and the path of Brazilian rates.
</p>

<h2 style="margin: 0 0 8px; font-size: 24px; font-weight: 800; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.2;">EQT to acquire Range Resources in all-stock deal — pro forma EV &gt;$50B, Marcellus/Utica dominance</h2>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">EQT buys Range Resources in all-stock deal valuing the combined natural-gas producer at a pro forma EV above $50 billion; close targeted Q1 2027.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
EQT Corporation and Range Resources signed a definitive merger agreement under which EQT acquires Range in an all-stock transaction. The combined company would be the largest US natural-gas producer, concentrated in the Marcellus and Utica shales of Appalachia, with pro forma enterprise value exceeding $50 billion.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
Each Range share converts into 0.987 EQT shares; EQT holders own ~73%, Range ~27% post-close. EQT guides to $325M of base annual synergies within two years (path to >$600M), ~$20B of cumulative FCF over five years, and combined production >9 Bcfe/d across ~3M net acres. Close targeted Q1 2027 subject to regulatory clearances and shareholder votes at both companies.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
This is the largest US upstream natural-gas tie-up since EQT's own 2024 Equitrans Midstream deal, creating a Marcellus-dominant supermajor just as LNG export capacity ramps on the Gulf Coast. Watch for antitrust review and peer M&A ripples in Antero (AR), CNX Resources (CNX), and Expand Energy (EXE).
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>
Energy desks, natural-gas commodity traders, and LNG strategists tracking Appalachian consolidation and Gulf Coast offtake dynamics.
</p>
