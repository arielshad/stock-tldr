<h2 style="margin: 0 0 8px; font-size: 24px; font-weight: 800; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.2;">Memory chip stocks rally — MU +5.8%, WDC +5.4%, SNDK +~5% after Lutnick says Trump admin opposes Apple buying Chinese memory</h2>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">Lutnick's 'not in favor' comment on Apple sourcing DRAM/NAND from CXMT and YMTC hands US memory names a demand tailwind.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
US Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick told the Wall Street Journal that the Trump administration is 'not in favor' of Apple purchasing memory chips from Chinese suppliers CXMT (DRAM) and YMTC (NAND). Apple had been testing CXMT DRAM and negotiating with YMTC for NAND for products sold outside the US.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
There is no formal rule — Lutnick said he had told Apple 'plainly' — but the signal alone moved the tape: Micron (MU) closed +5.8% near US$1,030, Western Digital (WDC) +5.4%, and SanDisk (SNDK) +~5% on the read that Apple's memory dollars flow to MU, Samsung and SK hynix instead.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
Apple is a top-3 memory buyer globally — redirecting even a slice of that demand to MU/Samsung/SK hynix meaningfully tightens an already AI-driven tight market. Counterpoint: no rule = no legal enforcement; CXMT/YMTC still supply the rest of the Android world.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>
Semis and memory traders, AI-capex allocators watching the NAND/DRAM tightness thesis into Micron's F4Q print in late September.
</p>

<h2 style="margin: 0 0 8px; font-size: 24px; font-weight: 800; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.2;">US 30-year Treasury yield tops 5.31% — highest since 2007 as deficits, sticky inflation and AI-capex issuance drive long-end selloff</h2>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">30Y Treasury yield hits 5.31% Monday, highest since 2007, as long-end sells off on deficits, inflation and AI-capex bond supply.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
The 30-year US Treasury yield climbed roughly six basis points to 5.31% on Monday Aug 17, breaching last month's high and reaching the loftiest since 2007. Long-end yields also rose in Canada (30Y highest since 2010) and Germany (rates at 2011 levels), reflecting a global reset in long-dated debt pricing.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
The move steepens the curve: the 2Y is down ~12bp month-to-date while the 30Y is up 13bp, widening the term premium. Drivers: mounting US government deficit, inflation stuck above the Fed's 2% target for five years, waning demand from traditional long-bond buyers, and fresh investment-grade issuance to fund AI capex. Barclays strategist Anshul Pradhan: 'We continue to argue against fading the long-end selloff.'
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
Rising real yields at the long end tighten financial conditions independently of the Fed — negative for long-duration equity (mega-cap tech, utilities) and 30Y mortgage rates. A close above 5.44% would eclipse the 2007 GFC-era peak. FOMC minutes this week and Jackson Hole Aug 27–29 are the next catalysts.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>
Rates traders, macro allocators, and anyone with duration exposure in long-bond ETFs like TLT or mega-cap tech names sensitive to discount-rate shifts.
</p>

<h2 style="margin: 0 0 8px; font-size: 24px; font-weight: 800; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.2;">US August 2026 Empire State Manufacturing 20.6 vs 11.0 est — fastest pace in 4+ years but prices paid jumps to 58.6</h2>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">Empire State factory index leaps to 20.6 in August — a 4-year high — but prices paid at 58.6 keeps inflation risk alive.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
The NY Fed's Empire State Manufacturing Survey jumped to 20.6 in August from 15.6 in July, nearly doubling the median economist forecast of 11.0 — its strongest reading in more than four years. NY Fed adviser Richard Deitz noted manufacturing activity 'increased at its fastest pace in over four years.'
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
Under the hood the picture is mixed: shipments cooled to 11.7 (from 24.4) and new orders eased to 17.3, yet unfilled orders jumped to 15.5 from 5.0, signalling backlogs building. Prices paid leapt to 58.6 from 52.3 — a key inflation flag — while the 6-month business-conditions index rose to 32.1 from 27.9.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
This is the first regional Fed survey before the September FOMC and it complicates the rate-cut story: real activity is hot AND input prices are re-accelerating on the same day the 30Y yield topped 5.31%. Philly Fed and manufacturing PMIs later this week are the next read.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>
Macro traders and rate desks watching September FOMC positioning; anyone modelling the September cut narrative against re-accelerating input costs.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>

His model shows the vehicle could accumulate up to ~$370B of senior debt by mid-2029 at 20-gigawatt scale. <p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">

Broadcom backstops customer lease payments capped at up to $29B on the initial tranche. The $370B is the maximum senior-debt stack XPV would carry at 20 GW — not Broadcom's own balance-sheet debt.

<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
AI-financing structures like XPV are the primary transmission channel for the AI capex cycle, enabling off-balance-sheet GPU deployment at scale. Curcuruto's note is the first credible sell-side pushback quantifying the debt trajectory — the leverage math and residual-value guarantees are what a downturn scenario would test. Watch AVGO credit spreads and read-through to APO and BX private-credit vehicles.

Alibaba has agreed to sell 100% of its gaming subsidiary Lingxi Games to Asia-focused PE firm Trustar Capital (formerly CITIC Capital). Lingxi CEO Zhou Bingshu disclosed the deal in an internal memo on Monday Aug 17 and will continue to run the studio post-sale.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
Reuters puts the price at more than US$2B; Bloomberg pegs it at at least US$1.5B. Alibaba framed the sale as part of a broader non-core asset review to free capital and focus on strategic AI and cloud infrastructure, where CapEx guidance has stepped up sharply through 2026.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
This is the clearest public signal that Alibaba's portfolio-cleanup thesis is executing — a real cash divestment, not a strategic review. It also puts a >US$1.5B tag on a Chinese mobile-games studio despite Tencent and NetEase still absorbing regulation cycles, and hands Trustar a growth asset ahead of Alibaba's next earnings test.
</p>

<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>
China tech investors and PE watchers tracking Alibaba's AI pivot and its implications for BABA's capital allocation heading into the Q1 FY27 print.
