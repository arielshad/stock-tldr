<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
The KOSPI is the global lead for the AI-memory trade — the rout echoed directly into US chip stocks with Micron −~6% and SanDisk −~9% on Friday. Monday's CXMT listing is now a binary: hot pricing validates the rotation out of Samsung/SK Hynix, soft pricing triggers a short squeeze back into Korean names.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>
Asia chip portfolio managers, memory long/shorts, macro EM desks, and AI-capex traders tracking the Samsung/SK Hynix vs. CXMT rotation.

<h2 style="margin: 0 0 8px; font-size: 24px; font-weight: 800; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.2;">Brent tops $100 for first time since May — +6.45% to $101.10 after Houthis strike Saudi tankers Encelia and Layla</h2>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">Houthi strikes on Saudi tankers push Brent above $100 for the first time since May, squeezing Saudi export routes from both the Red Sea and the Strait of Hormuz simultaneously.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
Yemen's Houthi movement struck two Saudi oil tankers — Encelia and Layla — in the Red Sea with missiles and drones on Thursday July 23, citing a blockade of Saudi ports and shipping. Brent closed +6.45% at $101.10, its first close above $100 since May 26; WTI rose 5.37% to $91.49.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
Five Saudi tankers reversed course in the Red Sea after the Houthi blockade declaration. Brent is now up ~40% for July as risk premiums expand across both the Red Sea and the Strait of Hormuz — with Hormuz already under a Trump-imposed toll since July 13, both Saudi export corridors are now under active pressure simultaneously.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
Oil at $100 flows directly into CPI and tightens the Fed's cutting path heading into next Wednesday's July 29 FOMC. Energy equities benefit, but airlines, refiners, and consumer discretionaries face margin compression — and the timing with new 12.5% Section 301 tariffs landing simultaneously is a two-way squeeze on inflation.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>
Energy desks, macro traders, tanker-shipping analysts, and anyone tracking the Fed's cut-path narrative into the July 29 FOMC.
</p>

<h2 style="margin: 0 0 8px; font-size: 24px; font-weight: 800; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.2;">Trump imposes Section 301 forced-labor tariffs on 60 trading partners — 12.5% on China and Vietnam, 10% on UK/Canada/Mexico; effective 12:01 a.m. ET Friday July 24</h2>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">New Section 301 duties on 60 trading partners replace the expiring flat 10% global baseline, covering ~99% of US imports under a two-tier labor-law framework.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
The Trump administration imposed tariffs of 10% to 12.5% on 60 US trading partners it accuses of failing to crack down on forced labor in supply chains, using Section 301 of US trade law. The duties took effect at 12:01 a.m. ET on Friday July 24 and replace the flat 10% global baseline tariff that expired the same morning.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
Most covered countries — including China and Vietnam — face 12.5%; a lower 10% applies to 17 countries with existing forced-labor prohibitions, including the UK, Canada and Mexico. The action covers ~99% of US imports with exemptions for oil and gas, steel, autos, and most USMCA-compliant goods.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
The step-up to 12.5% for most partners hit import prices the same day Brent crossed $100 — a two-way CPI squeeze one week before the July 29 FOMC. Affected partners rejected the forced-labor framing but signaled negotiation over retaliation, at least for now.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>
Trade desks, macro strategists, importers, and apparel and consumer-discretionary supply-chain analysts tracking the FOMC cut-path and inflationary overhang.
</p>

<h2 style="margin: 0 0 8px; font-size: 24px; font-weight: 800; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.2;">Apple (AAPL) +3.53% to ~$333 — Apple + Ford unveil MapKit for Automotive SDK for Ford's 2027 Universal EV Platform; Morgan Stanley raises PT to $364</h2>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">Ford becomes the launch partner for Apple's new MapKit for Automotive SDK, embedding Apple Maps natively into its 2027 Universal EV Platform independent of iPhone or CarPlay.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
Apple announced that Ford's Universal Electric Vehicle Platform — debuting in 2027 with a midsize electric pickup priced ~$30,000 out of Louisville Assembly in Kentucky — will use Apple Maps as its factory-embedded navigation via a newly disclosed MapKit for Automotive SDK. Ford is the first automaker in the world to deploy the SDK.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
MapKit for Automotive runs Apple Maps natively on the vehicle displays — including turn-by-turn directions, real-time traffic, intelligent EV routing, and battery preconditioning — independent of any connected iPhone. Ford's Latitude AI will feed Apple Maps road-level data into the next-generation BlueCruise hands-free system (Level 2 at launch in 2027, Level 3 targeted for 2028).
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
It's Apple's first meaningful auto-OS win since shuttering Project Titan in 2024, positioning Apple Maps as a direct competitor to Google Automotive Services used across Volvo/GM. Morgan Stanley's Erik Woodring raised the AAPL price target to $364 (from $360) and reiterated Buy.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>
Auto-tech analysts, AAPL longs, and autonomy watchers tracking the post-Project-Titan Apple roadmap and the Google Automotive Services competitive landscape.
</p>
