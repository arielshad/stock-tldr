<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">Reuters says Silver Lake has held discussions to buy Workday; WDAY posts best single-day gain on record at $206.45.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
Reuters reported Silver Lake has held discussions in recent months to take Workday private in what would be one of the largest software buyouts ever. Workday is the HR and financial-management SaaS vendor serving 11,500+ enterprise customers including Netflix and U.S. Bank.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
Workday entered the session at a ~$43B market cap; shares closed +17.78% at $206.45, implying a ~$51.1B post-news valuation. Silver Lake could bring in co-investors to finance the deal, similar to its earlier $55B Electronic Arts take-private with Affinity Partners and Saudi Arabia's PIF.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
A completed deal would rank among the largest software buyouts in history and signal PE conviction that traditional enterprise SaaS still has durable cash-flow runway. WDAY was down ~15% YTD and ~40% off its 2024 peak on fears that AI-native workflow tools chip away at core HR/finance software.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>
Enterprise-software investors and PE-arb desks watching for deal confirmation or collapse — the +17.78% pop on an unconfirmed rumor sets up a sharp binary event.
</p>

<h2 style="margin: 0 0 8px; font-size: 24px; font-weight: 800; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.2;">U.S. July retail sales -0.6% M/M — biggest monthly drop in over a year, well below +0.1% est</h2>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">The Census Bureau's advance July retail report shows total sales fell to $763.6B on broad weakness in autos and online — pulling the S&P off Thursday's record high.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
The Census Bureau's advance retail trade report for July 2026 showed headline sales fell 0.6% M/M to $763.6B — the biggest monthly drop in over a year — missing the Dow Jones consensus of +0.1%. Year-over-year growth stayed positive at +5.0%.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
Autos &amp; parts fell -1.8%, nonstore/online retailers -2.2%, and gasoline stations -0.9%. Bright spots were clothing (+1.9%) and health/personal care (+0.7%). The advance estimate carries a ±0.4pp sampling error.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
The miss, arriving alongside weak prelim UMich sentiment, pulled the S&P 500 off its record high to close -0.17% at 7,785.76. After tame CPI and PPI earlier in the week, it sharpens focus on the September FOMC path ahead of Jackson Hole (Aug 21–23).
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>
Macro traders and rate desks recalibrating the consumer picture heading into Jackson Hole, the Sept 16–17 FOMC, and the July PCE release on Aug 26.
</p>
