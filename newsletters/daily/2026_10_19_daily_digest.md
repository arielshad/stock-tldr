<h2 style="margin: 0 0 8px; font-size: 24px; font-weight: 800; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.2;">China Q3 2026 GDP +4.5% Y/Y — Misses ~4.8% Consensus</h2>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">China Q3 2026 GDP +4.5% Y/Y misses ~4.8% MNI preview as property investment dives 20.6% YTD and retail sales slow to +1.1%.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
China's National Bureau of Statistics released Q3 2026 GDP at +4.5% Y/Y, decelerating from Q2's +4.3% advance. Jan-Sep cumulative GDP totaled CNY 107,107.1 billion (+4.6% Y/Y) — tracking below Beijing's unofficial "around 5%" full-year target.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
The MNI pre-release advisor survey expected ~4.8%. Jan-Sep fixed-asset investment fell 7.2% Y/Y while real-estate development investment dropped 20.6%; September retail sales grew just 1.1% Y/Y while industrial value-added rose 5.5%.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
The two-speed economy sharpens: exports and factory output (+5.5%) are carrying what consumer demand (+1.1%) cannot. Property remains the structural drag — a 20.6% YTD contraction in real-estate development investment keeps the balance-sheet recession alive.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>
Macro traders, China/EM funds, and commodity desks watching FXI, MCHI, KWEB, iron-ore, and base metals for the Monday reopen reaction.
</p>

<h2 style="margin: 0 0 8px; font-size: 24px; font-weight: 800; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.2;">Trump Waives Lindsey Graham Russia-Iran Sanctions Act Two Days Before It Took Effect</h2>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">Trump signs EO Fri Oct 16 waiving nearly all of the new Lindsey O. Graham Russia-Iran sanctions act two days before it was due to kick in.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
President Trump signed an executive order late Friday Oct 16 waiving nearly all substantive sanctions, tariffs and restrictions in the Lindsey O. Graham Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act — the same law he had signed only a month earlier on Sept 18. The waiver came two days before the Russia provisions were set to take effect on Oct 18.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
The Graham Act (Senate 86-11, House 262-159) imposed tariffs of up to 500% on Russian goods, up to 100% on the top five buyers of Russian crude and gas, secondary sanctions on foreign banks, and bans on U.S. purchases of Russian sovereign debt. The EO waives all of these provisions plus the requirement that existing sanctions stay in place.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
The second Russia sanctions waiver in two weeks — far broader than the Oct 9 diesel waiver — drew sharp bipartisan backlash (Haley: "terrible misjudgment"; Cruz: "I wish he hadn't done it"; Tillis: "beyond irresponsible"). Russia's Kirill Dmitriev called it "an important and truly historic moment."
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>
Energy desks, geopolitical risk traders, and FX desks tracking Brent crude, Russian ruble exposure, and European energy security.
</p>

<h2 style="margin: 0 0 8px; font-size: 24px; font-weight: 800; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.2;">Rocket Lab (RKLB) Delays Neutron Maiden Launch to 2027 — Stock -16% in Two Sessions</h2>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">Neutron slips to 2027 — carbon-composite dome defect pushes Rocket Lab's SpaceX chase out another year.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
Rocket Lab filed an 8-K on Oct 14 saying its Neutron medium-lift reusable launcher — meant to compete with SpaceX's Falcon 9 — will make its inaugural flight in 2027, not Q4 2026. The cause is a manufacturing defect (voids) in the carbon-composite forward dome of Neutron's first-stage propellant tank. This is Neutron's fourth slip since its original 2024 target.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
Engineers found air pockets in the carbon-composite forward dome; Rocket Lab reproduced the defect in testing and has changed the manufacturing process. Full first-stage assembly is now expected in Q1 2027, with a firm launch date to follow. CEO Peter Beck stated "possible success isn't our standard."
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
RKLB fell ~7.85% intraday to $59.18 then another ~9.94% after hours to $53.30 — a ~16% two-day drawdown that leaves the stock ~65% below its 52-week high of $151. The slip extends SpaceX's effective monopoly on US medium-lift reusable launch for another year.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>
Space-sector investors, defense and launch desks, and anyone tracking the competitive dynamics in US medium-lift launch.
</p>
