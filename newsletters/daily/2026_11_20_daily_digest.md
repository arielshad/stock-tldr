<h2 style="margin: 0 0 8px; font-size: 24px; font-weight: 800; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.2;">US Stocks Slide for 6th Straight Day — S&amp;P 500 -2.02%, Nasdaq -2.25%, All 11 Sectors Red</h2>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">Sixth straight down session for the S&amp;P 500 — the longest losing streak since April 2024 — as an early Nvidia-fueled rally flips into a broad risk-off.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
Thursday Nov 19 2026 closed as a broad US equity rout. The S&amp;P 500 shed 147.80 points to 7,175.83 (-2.02%), its sixth straight decline and longest losing streak since April 2024. The Dow fell 1,101 points to 49,097.06 (-2.19%) and the Nasdaq dropped 550.69 points to 23,879.20 (-2.25%). Every one of the 11 S&amp;P 500 sectors finished lower.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
The session reversed sharply from an early rally on Nvidia's prior-night beat. Nvidia swung from +4.7% intraday to close -3.3% at $145.98; AMD fell -7.72% on a report alleging ties to a blacklisted Chinese server maker. Williams-Sonoma -15.16% was the worst S&amp;P 500 name; Palo Alto Networks +6.42% was the best. VIX spiked to 28.44 and 10-year yields slipped to 5.052%.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
A bellwether AI-capex beat (Nvidia) failing to hold the market is a first this cycle — signaling positioning cleanup is still working through. Breadth is deteriorating: equal-weight S&amp;P only -1.76%, meaning this is broader than just Mag Seven, and China/export-control risk on semis remains live heading into the holiday.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>
US equity index traders, semiconductor desks, discretionary PMs watching the longest losing streak since April 2024.
</p>

<h2 style="margin: 0 0 8px; font-size: 24px; font-weight: 800; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.2;">Anthropic (ANT) Opens at $111.50 on Nasdaq Debut — +34% from $83 IPO Price, Briefly ~$2T Market Cap</h2>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">Largest IPO in history: Anthropic opens 34% above its $83 price, briefly pushing market cap above $2T before fading to $97.17 at close (+17.1%).</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
Anthropic PBC, maker of the Claude family of AI models, began trading on Nasdaq under ticker ANT on Thursday November 19, 2026. Shares were priced at $83 — above the $70–$78 range — with 980M Class A shares sold for $81.3B in proceeds, surpassing SpaceX's June 2026 debut as the largest IPO in history.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
The first trade printed at $111.50 (+34%) around 11:48 a.m. ET, with an intraday high of $114.99 briefly implying a ~$2T market cap — the sixth most valuable U.S. company behind Apple, Microsoft, Nvidia, Alphabet, and Amazon. Shares faded to $97.17 at close (+17.1%). Only ~7.4% of total shares trade publicly; Nvidia, Microsoft, Google, Broadcom, and AMD bought a combined ~$26B at IPO price in a concurrent private placement.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
The debut cements AI as the dominant 2026 IPO theme and reprices the private AI market at a public-market scale on par with the mega-cap tech platforms Anthropic sells into. Nasdaq-100 inclusion is set for December 18; S&amp;P 500 eligibility is at least a year away.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>
AI investors, IPO allocators, growth equity, and anyone tracking the mega-cap AI valuation benchmark for OpenAI and future AI lab listings.
</p>

<h2 style="margin: 0 0 8px; font-size: 24px; font-weight: 800; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.2;">Williams-Sonoma (WSM) -15.16% — Q3 FY26 Beat Everywhere, But FY Guidance Only Reiterated</h2>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">Williams-Sonoma beats across the board but reiterates — not raises — the FY outlook, and the stock takes its worst session of the year as the single worst S&amp;P 500 performer.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
Williams-Sonoma reported Q3 FY26 (quarter ended Nov 1 2026) before the open. Net revenue rose 5.8% to $1,991.2M; comparable brand revenue was +5.1%. Non-GAAP diluted EPS was $2.19 (+11.7%), GAAP EPS $2.38 (+21.4%). Every brand posted a positive comp: West Elm +7.0%, Williams Sonoma +6.8%, Pottery Barn +1.8%.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
The critical miss: management reiterated — did not raise — its FY26 outlook of +4.7–7.2% revenue growth, +4.0–6.5% comps, and 17.8–18.2% non-GAAP operating margin. GAAP gross margin of 47.6% included roughly 140bp from IEEPA tariff refunds, making clean organic EPS growth less impressive than the headline +21.4%. The company returned $253M in Q3 via $163M in buybacks and $90M in dividends.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
The size of the drawdown on a clean operational quarter tells you the buy-side priced in a holiday-driven guidance raise that never came. Read-throughs to RH, Wayfair, and the luxury home complex are mixed — comp momentum is intact but the sell-off signals rising conviction that higher-end consumer demand is already fully priced in.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>
Home furnishings and discretionary desks, tariff-tailwind trackers, mid-cap retail PMs watching the holiday setup for the luxury home complex.
</p>

<h2 style="margin: 0 0 8px; font-size: 24px; font-weight: 800; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.2;">Walmart (WMT) Q3 FY27 — Revenue $188.8B (+5.2%), Adj EPS $0.65 Beat, FY27 Outlook Raised</h2>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">Walmart tops Q3 FY27 and raises the FY27 outlook, but shares dip premarket as margin pressure and higher capex overshadow the small beat.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
Walmart reported fiscal Q3 2027 (quarter ended Oct 31 2026) before the bell. Total revenue was $188.8B (+5.2% reported, +4.5% constant currency) with adjusted EPS of $0.65, slightly above the ~$0.64 Street consensus. The company raised its full-year FY27 guidance for sales, operating income, and EPS.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
Walmart U.S. comps ex-fuel rose 2.9%; Sam's Club U.S. comps were +4.9%. Global eCommerce grew 17% and is now 24% of net sales; U.S. advertising surged +37% and marketplace/fulfillment services +49%. FY27 adj EPS guidance raised to $2.84–$2.89 (from $2.80–$2.87) and net sales cc growth to 4.9–5.2% (from 4.0–5.0%). Capex guidance increased to ~4.0% of net sales.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
Walmart is the U.S. retail bellwether and the message is consistent: the consumer is "stable and resilient." High-margin businesses (advertising, marketplace) are outgrowing the core store, explaining why operating income growth is running ahead of sales growth. The soft premarket reaction reflects investor focus on margin pressure and higher capex — free cash flow is down ~23% through nine months.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>
Retail analysts, consumer macro desks tracking the health of the U.S. consumer, and Target/Costco comp watchers heading into the holiday season.
</p>

<h2 style="margin: 0 0 8px; font-size: 24px; font-weight: 800; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.2;">Palo Alto Networks (PANW) Q1 FY27 — Revenue +39% to $3.44B, NGS ARR +66%, FY27 Guide Raised, Acquires Applied Compute</h2>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">PANW beat Q1 FY27 across every line, raised FY27 guidance materially, and announced plans to buy open-weight-AI inference platform Applied Compute — best S&amp;P 500 name on Nov 19.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
Palo Alto Networks reported fiscal Q1 2027 (quarter ended Oct 31 2026) after the Wednesday close. Revenue of $3.44B grew 39% Y/Y — the strongest growth in multiple years — beating the ~$3.31B consensus. Non-GAAP diluted EPS came in at $1.05 against the ~$0.97 estimate; Next-Generation Security ARR hit $9.73B (+66% Y/Y).
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
Remaining Performance Obligations reached $21.0B (+35% Y/Y). FY27 revenue guidance was raised to $14.52–$14.57B (+26–27%) and non-GAAP EPS to $4.26–$4.30 at a 29.5% non-GAAP operating margin. PANW also announced plans to acquire Applied Compute, a post-training and inference platform for open-weight AI models, at an undisclosed price.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
This is cybersecurity's most important platformization read of the quarter: NGS ARR growth re-accelerated to +66% and cash flow with backlog both expanded through a broader tech wobble. The Applied Compute deal extends PANW into the open-weight AI inference stack, directly competing with CrowdStrike, Zscaler, and Cloudflare on AI-security.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>
Software and cybersecurity analysts, platform-sector allocators, and anyone tracking the AI-security build-out versus CrowdStrike (CRWD), Zscaler (ZS), and Cloudflare (NET).
</p>

<h2 style="margin: 0 0 8px; font-size: 24px; font-weight: 800; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.2;">Ross Stores (ROST) Q3 FY26 — Sales $6.3B (+12%), Comps +8% on Traffic, EPS $1.91 Crushes Guide, FY26 Raised</h2>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">Ross beats the Q3 guide by a dime, raises FY EPS, and completes its 115-store 2026 plan — but AH sellers fade the print, with shares -5.2% after hours.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
Ross Stores reported Q3 FY26 (13 weeks ended Oct 31 2026) after the close Thursday. Total sales rose 12% to $6.3B with comparable store sales up 8% — on top of a +7% gain a year earlier — driven mainly by customer traffic. Diluted EPS was $1.91, clearing the $1.75–$1.83 guidance; operating margin reached 12.3% (+70bp Y/Y).
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
Ross opened 51 new stores in the quarter (41 Ross Dress for Less + 10 dd's DISCOUNTS), completing its 115-store 2026 plan and ending with 2,377 locations. The company repurchased 1.4M shares for $319M in Q3, on track for $1.275B in FY26 buybacks. FY26 EPS guidance was raised to $8.75–$8.84 (from $8.61–$8.77). Q4 outlook: comps +4–5%, EPS $2.16–$2.25.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
A traffic-driven +8% comp on top of last year's +7% is a strong read for the off-price trade into the holiday. The AH -5.2% fade suggests sell-the-news positioning after a strong YTD run; the modest FY guide lift ($0.14 at the midpoint) and the jumpy discretionary tape (WSM -15%) both set a cautious tone for Friday's open. Read-across to TJX (BURL) into holiday.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>
Off-price retail PMs, consumer discretionary desks tracking the holiday comp setup for TJX and Burlington.
</p>
