<h2 style="margin: 0 0 8px; font-size: 24px; font-weight: 800; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.2;">Dow Erases 1,100-Point Intraday Loss — Thanksgiving-Eve Comeback</h2>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">Thanksgiving-eve risk-off at the open, aggressively bought back by the close as the Dow erases a 1,100-pt drop.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
US equities staged a dramatic late-day comeback on Wed Nov 25, the last full session before Thanksgiving. The Dow sank more than 1,100 points intraday on hot core PCE, AI-overcapex worries, and new tariff threats — before buyers turned the tape in the afternoon.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
Dow closed +0.33% at 50,182.95 after being down ~1,100 pts. S&P 500 finished -0.25% at 7,182.78 (off a -3.2% intraday low). Nasdaq -0.62% to 23,585.80. VIX ran to ~35 intraday, closing at 28.29. Chips led the selling — Nvidia fell 8% intraday before closing -4%. HPQ surged ~+11% on its Q4 beat.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
The shape of the day — a 3%+ intraday rip in both directions — signals how fast risk is swinging now that the AI-capex trade has cracked and December Fed pricing is locked in for a hike. With markets closed Thursday and a half-day Friday, next week's catalysts (Dec 1 Nvidia hearings, Dec 4 jobs, Dec 8–9 FOMC) will decide whether Wednesday afternoon is a floor or a bull trap.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>
US equity index traders, volatility sellers, short-duration rates desks.
</p>

<h2 style="margin: 0 0 8px; font-size: 24px; font-weight: 800; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.2;">October PCE +3.8% Y/Y — December Rate Hike Now 97.8% Priced</h2>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">October PCE reaccelerates to +3.8% Y/Y headline (highest since 2023) and +3.3% Y/Y core — FedWatch pricing ~97.8% odds of a 25bp December rate hike.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
The BEA released October 2026 Personal Income and Outlays on Nov 25. Headline PCE rose +0.3% M/M and +3.8% Y/Y — the fastest Y/Y pace since 2023, matching consensus. Core PCE (ex food and energy) rose +0.3% M/M and +3.3% Y/Y, slightly above the ~3.2% estimate and the highest core reading in three years. September was revised UP on both headline and core.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
Personal income jumped +0.7% M/M (+$201.8B). Nominal spending rose +0.3% but real PCE fell $5.8B — the nominal gain was more than entirely prices. The personal saving rate climbed to 4.1% (from 3.6%). This marks the sixth consecutive month the Fed's preferred gauge has held above 3%.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
A hot print plus an upward revision locks in the hawkish setup for the December FOMC — CME FedWatch is pricing a 97.8% probability of a 25bp hike at Dec 8–9. Real spending going negative while prices accelerate is stagflation-lite, undercutting any dovish case. This is the last PCE print the FOMC sees before deciding.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>
Macro traders, rates desks, Fed-watchers.
</p>

<h2 style="margin: 0 0 8px; font-size: 24px; font-weight: 800; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.2;">Dell Q3 FY27: $60B AI Orders, Revenue +103%, FY27 Guide Raised to $202B</h2>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">Dell Q3 doubles revenue to $54.9B on $60B of quarterly AI orders — FY27 guide raised to $202B and +$25B buyback.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
Dell Technologies reported fiscal Q3 FY27 (Nov 24 after close), posting a record quarter driven by hyperscaler AI-server demand. Revenue was $54.94B (+103% Y/Y), non-GAAP EPS $9.45 (+265%), both well ahead of LSEG consensus (~$6.59 est). The company raised FY27 guidance by $10B on the top line to $202B.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
Infrastructure Solutions Group revenue was $41.97B (+198%), with AI-optimized server revenue a record $22.03B (+291%). Dell booked $60.2B of AI orders in Q3 and exited with $132.3B of AI backlog. The board authorized an additional $25B share repurchase. Q4 FY27 guide is $56.3B revenue and $8.40 non-GAAP EPS.

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
Dell's print landed the same day the SOX fell 7.9% on AI-margin anxiety following HPE's report. The scale of Dell's AI order book and the guide raise argue the capex cycle is still accelerating even as hardware peers warn on margin compression. Shares closed -11.8% at $295.28 before the beat; AH quotes ranged +1.5% to +6% as the magnitude of the guide raise filtered in.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">HP's FY26 print beats its own guide on PC strength and tariff refunds; stock pops ~11% on raised dividend and FY27 EPS floor of $3.15.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">

</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
Personal Systems revenue of $11.8B jumped 14%, with Commercial PS +23% driven by HP's AI-PC mix. Printing revenue slipped 1% to $4.3B. FY27 guide is non-GAAP EPS $3.15–$3.45 and FCF $2.9–$3.1B; starting Q1 FY27, HP will exclude stock-based comp from non-GAAP metrics (~$0.38 reduction to the comp base).

<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
HPQ led the S&P 500 on Nov 25, up ~11% while the Nasdaq was still red, reframing PC demand after the chip-sector rout. The FY27 EPS floor of $3.15 and dividend bump to $0.31 (+3.3%) keep HP in the value/capital-return basket favored when investors rotate out of high-multiple semis.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">Edge-AI chip designer slashes Q4 revenue guide to $70–$75M (~38% below consensus) as memory scarcity intensifies across its customer base.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
Ambarella reported fiscal Q3 FY27 (Nov 24 after close), posting results at the low end of the guide and then shocking the Street with a Q4 outlook roughly 38% below consensus. CEO Fermi Wang cited escalating memory prices and supply scarcity as customers cut end-product plans across consumer IoT, automotive, and enterprise IoT.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
Q3 revenue was $115.2M (+6.2% Y/Y) versus the $115–$124M guide; non-GAAP EPS $0.28. The Q4 FY27 outlook is $70–$75M revenue — down ~38% Q/Q and vs ~$112M consensus. CEO Wang said "the scarcity of memory supply we previously highlighted has intensified" and that customers are reducing outlooks across all markets.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
Ambarella is the first chip-designer to cut a quarter outright on the HBM/DDR5 crunch driving AI capex — the same supply tightness HPE flagged for its FY27 margin compression. Expect read-across to other memory-exposed edge/mid-tier chip names and consumer IoT OEMs. AMBA closed -20% at $44.47 then fell another 37–44% AH.
</p>
