<h2 style="margin: 0 0 8px; font-size: 24px; font-weight: 800; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.2;">Nasdaq -2.6% — Tech Rout + Oil Spike on Iran War</h2>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">Tech stocks got smacked as AI-spending fears collided with an oil spike on fresh Iran-war worries.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
The Nasdaq Composite fell 2.6% to 26,008.86 and the S&P 500 shed 1.2% to 7,663.43 on Thursday Oct 22 in a split-tape risk-off session. The VIX jumped 14% to 19.03 as Meta, Tesla, semis, and AI infrastructure names sold off hard.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
Meta closed down ~7% after its Q3 beat was overshadowed by a $9.89B teen-safety settlement and a $135–145B FY26 capex guide; Intel fell ~8%, CoreWeave ~10%, Lam Research ~9%. Separately, the NYT reported US-Israel plans for strikes on Iran after Nov 3, pushing Brent past $112 and WTI to a 5-month high near $100.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
This is the first clear test of whether mega-cap AI prints can keep carrying the tape or trigger a vol regime change. With a renewed Iran-war oil premium layered on top, the macro backdrop stops being a one-way street heading into AAPL/MSFT/GOOGL/AMZN prints next week.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>
Tech/semi traders, macro desks, energy investors.
</p>

<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>

</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>

Europe parts organic revenue fell 9.8% with segment EBITDA margin of just 4.1–5.1% (vs 10.0% Y/Y) due to a Germany ERP disruption. <p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
LKQ is the dominant aftermarket parts platform in North America and Europe — the ERP rollout cratered a decade-old European margin base and the Board responded with a full leadership reset and an outside restructuring adviser. Watch the full Q3 release on Oct 29 for cash flow, debt, and European write-down detail.

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">Record quarter and third FY26 guide hike, but a sudden swing to negative operating cash flow and a 3x capex ramp sent BE down 11% the next session.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
Bloom Energy, the solid-oxide fuel-cell maker supplying AI data centers via Nebius, Brookfield, Oracle, and Equinix, posted record Q3 2026 revenue of $1,235.8M (+138.1% Y/Y) with non-GAAP EPS of $0.94. The company raised FY26 guidance for the third time this year and announced it was added to the S&P 500.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
Raised FY26 guidance: revenue $4.4–$4.6B (from $3.9–$4.2B), non-GAAP EPS $3.10–$3.40 (from $2.55–$2.85). The catch: operating cash flow swung to -$27.1M (from +$226.4M in Q2) as capex tripled to $153.5M to fund capacity expansion, yielding implied FCF of about -$181M.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
BE is the cleanest listed proxy for AI data-center power delivery outside big-cap utilities. The FCF pivot signals that fuel-cell AI power is as capex-heavy as GPU hyperscaler build-outs — a structural re-rating for the whole AI power infrastructure complex (VST, CEG, TLN, PLUG, FCEL).
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">Bloomberg says SpaceX wants ≥80K US cell sites to launch a fourth nationwide carrier — tower REITs rip, carriers get hit.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
Bloomberg reported Thursday after close that SpaceX is in talks with Crown Castle, American Tower, and Vertical Bridge to lease at least 80,000 US cell sites. The move would complement Starlink's direct-to-cell satellite network and points toward SpaceX entering the US wireless carrier market.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
Crown Castle (CCI) surged ~12% to ~$87 Friday; American Tower (AMT) rose ~7%. AT&amp;T (T) fell ~5% and Verizon (VZ) ~3%. SpaceX already holds FCC approval for ~15,000 Starlink D2C satellites and recently closed an ~$8B 800 MHz spectrum purchase from Grain Management.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
If SpaceX leases ground sites at scale alongside Starlink D2C, it reintroduces a credible fourth-carrier threat for the first time since the Sprint/T-Mobile merger. Bernstein's Laurent Yoon noted: "Committing to 80K macro sites could be a point of no return."
</p>

<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>
Telecom analysts, tower REIT holders, Starlink watchers.
