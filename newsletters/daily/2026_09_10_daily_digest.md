<h2 style="margin: 0 0 8px; font-size: 24px; font-weight: 800; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.2;">Trump signs 5 Section 338 proclamations banning Canadian alcohol, dairy, and motorcycles — effective Sept 29</h2>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">US bans most Canadian booze, dairy, and larger motorcycles from import starting Sept 29 in escalation of the North American trade war.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
President Trump signed five proclamations under Section 338 of the Tariff Act of 1930 — an obscure 95-year-old statute permitting outright exclusion of goods from countries the president finds are "discriminating" against US commerce. It's the sharpest escalation since 50% Section 338 duties on ~$20B of Canadian goods took effect Aug 22.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
Full import bans take effect Sept 29 on most Canadian alcoholic beverages, dairy (whey, molasses), and larger motorcycles/mopeds. Separate tariff modifications effective Sept 15 add all-terrain vehicles and animal hides while removing rock salt and cement. The trigger: Canada's retaliatory 15–50% duties on ~$20B of US goods took effect at 12:01 a.m. Eastern the same day.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
Section 338 hasn't seriously been used since the 1940s; full import bans (not just duties) are a material escalation and a template Washington could apply to other partners. Direct exposure: Diageo (DEO), Constellation Brands (STZ), Molson Coors (TAP), Harley-Davidson (HOG), and Canadian dairy processors.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>
Macro traders, consumer-staples desks, and USD/CAD traders — further pressure on the loonie, front-end rates, and any US inflation print sensitive to tradable goods.
</p>
