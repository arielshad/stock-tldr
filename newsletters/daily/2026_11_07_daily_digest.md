<h2 style="margin: 0 0 8px; font-size: 24px; font-weight: 800; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.2;">US October Jobs: +165K vs +59K est — Dec Fed Hike Odds Jump to 78%</h2>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">Hot October payrolls — +165K vs +59K est, unemployment 4.1% — push December FOMC hike odds to 78%.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
The BLS October 2026 Employment Situation dropped Friday at 8:30 a.m. ET — nonfarm payrolls rose +165K vs Dow Jones consensus of +59K, the unemployment rate ticked down to 4.1% from 4.2%, and Aug/Sep were revised up a combined +84K.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
Average hourly earnings rose +0.4% m/m (+3.1% y/y) to $38.00; professional/business services led (+70K), followed by healthcare (+42K) and transportation (+25K). Beneath the headline, long-term unemployment worsened — avg duration jumped to 27.1 weeks and U-6 rose 0.3pt to 7.9%.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
The Fed is already holding at 3.75–4.00% with two dissenters pushing for a hike — this print tightens the path: CME FedWatch Dec hike odds jumped to 78% from 70%. The 2Y yield rose 6bp to 4.52%, the 10Y added 3bp to 4.87%, and the DXY gained 0.7% to 102.22.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>
Macro traders, rates desks, and Fed-watchers ahead of the Dec 8–9 FOMC meeting.
</p>

<h2 style="margin: 0 0 8px; font-size: 24px; font-weight: 800; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.2;">Moderna (MRNA) Q3 2026: Revenue $1.313B (+29% Y/Y), FY26 Guide Raised to 15–25% Growth — Stock +10% Premarket</h2>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">Moderna Q3 revenue $1.313B (+29% Y/Y), raises FY26 growth outlook to 15–25% and lifts year-end cash guide to $8.1–$8.3B.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
Moderna's Q3 2026 results (before Nov 6 open): total revenue $1.313B (+29% Y/Y), GAAP net income $162M, and GAAP diluted EPS $0.38 vs a $(0.51) loss per share a year earlier. The company also raised FY26 revenue growth to 15–25% from the prior up-to-10%.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
U.S. revenue was $780M and international $533M; Q3 cost of sales rose 54% Y/Y to $318M while R&D fell 27% to $583M. The quarter also included positive Phase 3 data for intismeran in resected high-risk melanoma (42% reduction in recurrence/death risk vs KEYTRUDA alone) and August FDA approval of mFLUSIVA.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
The lifted FY26 revenue range, cost discipline, and raised cash guide ($8.1–$8.3B, from $4.7–$5.2B) push out the solvency concerns that dogged MRNA through 2025–mid-2026. The melanoma readout and mFLUSIVA add two nearer-term revenue levers beyond COVID.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>
Biotech specialists and mRNA-platform traders watching the post-COVID pivot play out in the numbers.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">Airbnb Q3 revenue $4.87B (+19%) and EPS $3.16 top ests, nights +16% fastest in 4 years, new $12B buyback; shares +11% AH.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
Airbnb reported Q3 2026 after the Nov 5 close: revenue $4.869B (+19% Y/Y), GAAP diluted EPS $3.16, and 155.0M nights and seats booked (+16% Y/Y) — the fastest Q3 growth in over three years. Management raised full-year guidance and authorized a new $12B share repurchase.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
Gross booking value reached $27.7B (+21%), adj EBITDA $2.509B at a 52% margin, and FCF $1.426B (29% margin). Q4 2026 guide calls for revenue $3.22–$3.28B (+16–18%); FY26 outlook raised to high-teens revenue growth and ~36% adj EBITDA margin.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
Nights-booked growth re-accelerated — the single metric bears had focused on — while rival BKNG and EXPE guided more cautiously into Q4, suggesting Airbnb is taking share. The new $12B authorization is roughly 10% of market cap and resets the buyback runway.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>
Travel equity desks and consumer-discretionary investors tracking the online travel cycle into year-end.

<h2 style="margin: 0 0 8px; font-size: 24px; font-weight: 800; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.2;">AST SpaceMobile (ASTS) + Firefly Aerospace ~$1.3B Deal: Miranda Engine License + 36 Engines for Blacklift Rocket — Firefly +30%, ASTS +6%</h2>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">AST SpaceMobile licenses Firefly's Miranda engine IP and takes 36 engines to power its in-house Blacklift rocket in a ~$1.3B deal.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
AST SpaceMobile disclosed a ~$1.3B agreement on Nov 6, 2026, under which Firefly Aerospace will supply 36 Miranda rocket engines and license the engine IP to AST — enabling AST to build its own 20-ton Blacklift launch vehicle for the BlueBird direct-to-cell constellation. Firefly surged ~30% and ASTS rose ~6%.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
AST pays ~$800M upfront for a perpetual license to manufacture Miranda and vacuum-variant Vira engines ($400M cash + 10–14M ASTS Class A shares), then $250M for engine delivery across the first three Blacklift launches and $250M for three years of engineering support. Miranda has logged 175+ hot-fire tests for 4,200+ seconds.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
The deal is a rare vertical-integration move by a satellite operator onto a major rocket engine, changing the launch-cost arithmetic for BlueBird while shifting execution risk onto an unproven in-house program. For Firefly, it's a large cash + equity anchor that validates Miranda outside internal use.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>
Space-sector investors and launch supply-chain watchers tracking the direct-to-cell satellite buildout.
</p>
