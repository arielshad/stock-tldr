<h2 style="margin: 0 0 8px; font-size: 24px; font-weight: 800; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.2;">Treasury Secretary Bessent launches 'Operation Economic Outcast' — OFAC hits 60+ entities across UAE, Hong Kong, China, Singapore and Switzerland to squeeze Iran's oil, shipping, gold, aviation, tech and digital-asset lifelines</h2>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">Bessent's 'Operation Economic Outcast' — OFAC hits 60+ enablers in 5 jurisdictions to sever every Iranian oil, gold, tech and crypto lifeline.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent held a Monday afternoon press conference announcing 'Operation Economic Outcast', an escalated secondary-sanctions campaign the administration frames as a global 'economic D-Day' against Iran. OFAC designated more than 60 entities, individuals and vessels based in the UAE, Hong Kong, mainland China, Singapore and Switzerland across oil and shipping, gold, aviation, technology, and digital assets.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
The order expands the categories eligible for US secondary sanctions on anyone doing business with Iran, and names specific Iranian bank Bank Melli plus foreign exchange houses. Bessent said Trump is personally calling world leaders with 'specific requests' to cut ties, and that a major foreign financial institution will be sanctioned 'later this week'.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
WTI settled -2.4% at $85.01 and Brent -2.4% at $92.17 as traders judged the sanctions well-telegraphed and stopped short of hitting a top-tier Chinese bank. Longer-term, secondary risk now sits on top-3 Iranian oil buyers (Chinese refiners), gold-clearing houses in Switzerland/HK, and shipping insurers touching Hormuz cargoes.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>
Energy desks, macro traders, EM credit, gold books.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">Trump doubles Canada auto tariff to 50% and slaps a new 50% duty on auto parts, effective Jan 1, 2027.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
President Trump posted on Truth Social Monday Aug 24 that the US will double tariffs on 'all Cars, Trucks, both large and small, Automotive Parts, and Steel' from Canada to 50%, effective Jan 1, 2027. The escalation lands 48 hours after Section 338 tariffs on ~$20B of Canadian goods went live, and after weekend negotiations to trim the auto rate to 15% collapsed on Sunday.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
Autos and light trucks currently face a 25% duty; the announcement doubles that to 50%. Auto parts get a new 50% duty from a base of 0%, and steel stays at the 50% level already imposed Aug 22.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
The Big Three (F, GM, STLA) run deeply integrated cross-border assembly through Windsor and Oshawa; a 50% duty on both finished vehicles and parts breaks the landed-cost stack for pickup trucks and SUVs assembled in Canada. Ontario Premier Doug Ford threatened counter-measures including restrictions on electricity and critical-mineral exports to the US.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>
Auto sector traders, USD/CAD desks, steel and battery-material books.
</p>

<h2 style="margin: 0 0 8px; font-size: 24px; font-weight: 800; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.2;">US-Canada trade talks collapse — 50% Section 338 tariffs on ~$20B of Canadian goods effective at midnight Aug 22, Carney vows dollar-for-dollar retaliation</h2>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">First-ever Section 338 tariffs — 50% duties on ~$20B of Canadian goods live after Friday-night talks collapse.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
US-Canada trade negotiations collapsed shortly before a midnight Friday deadline, sending Section 338 tariffs live at 12:01 a.m. ET on Saturday Aug 22. The duties add a 50% surcharge on roughly $20 billion of Canadian imports — cement, softwood lumber, hockey sticks, certain building materials, liquors, and specific apparel.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
Imposed under Section 338 of the Tariff Act of 1930 — a statute never previously used by a president — the 50% duty lets the White House hit any trading partner that 'discriminates' against US commerce. Trump had originally set the tariffs live Aug 19, then paused them 3 days for negotiation before talks collapsed.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
This first presidential use of Section 338 opens a new legal channel for tariff action after the Supreme Court struck down earlier IEEPA-based duties in February. Canadian retaliation on the same $20B scale now escalates the trade war heading into the Labor Day week and Jackson Hole.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>
Macro traders, autos and homebuilder desks, USD/CAD.
</p>

<h2 style="margin: 0 0 8px; font-size: 24px; font-weight: 800; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.2;">S&amp;P Global US Flash Composite PMI August 2026 — 56.0, 4-year high; Services jumps to 56.8 (fastest since Dec 2024), Manufacturing slips to 53.2 (13-month low); Q3 GDP tracking ~3% annualized</h2>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">US private-sector activity hit its fastest pace since April 2022 in August, all on the services side.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
S&amp;P Global's flash Purchasing Managers' Index for August came in at a composite 56.0, up from 54.5 in July — the strongest US business expansion in more than four years. Services (56.8) beat the 54.0 consensus; manufacturing (53.2) missed 53.9 estimates and cooled to a 13-month low.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
PMI values above 50 signal expansion; the composite blends manufacturing output and services activity. Services ran at its fastest since December 2024 while goods production posted its weakest increase in 13 months, partly on fading safety-stock building and supply-chain delays lengthening to their worst in about four years.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
S&amp;P Global's chief economist called it 'US business booming, with the fastest output growth for over four years,' consistent with Q3 GDP tracking ~3% annualized versus 1.5% in Q2. A strong growth print five days before Chair Warsh's Jackson Hole keynote reduces near-term Fed easing odds — 10Y yields ticked higher on the data while equities held (S&amp;P 500 +0.43%).
</p>
