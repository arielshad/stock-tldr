<h2 style="margin: 0 0 8px; font-size: 24px; font-weight: 800; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.2;">Nasdaq -2.86% Fri Oct 30 — worst day since June, worst month since March 2025</h2>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">AI-earnings fallout crushes Intel, Reddit, and Roblox as the market reprices mega-cap capex after Amazon and Alphabet already stung mega-caps earlier in the week.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
The Nasdaq Composite closed down 2.86% (747 points) at 25,437 on Friday October 30 — its worst single session since June and worst calendar month since March 2025. The S&P 500 fell 1.82% to 7,580 and the Dow dropped 1.09% to 51,770, extending a two-day AI-earnings rout.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
Intel (INTC) cratered 28.58% on a weak Q4 guide with PC weakness flagged through H1 2027; Reddit fell ~29% as US DAU declined Y/Y for the first time since its IPO; Roblox tumbled 16–18% on a 9% DAU drop. Two Fed officials on Thursday pushed for higher rates, while ExxonMobil and Chevron bucked the selloff with record-refining earnings.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
The AI-capex-funded September/October rally hit its first real stress test — mega-cap AI spenders are now penalized for the scale of their build, not rewarded. With the Fed in hawkish hold, growth multiples are less forgiving heading into Apple (Nov 2) and Nvidia (Nov 19) earnings.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>
Equity index traders, tech sector allocators, and momentum/volatility traders navigating the post-AI-capex repricing and the Fed's hawkish-hold backdrop.
</p>

<h2 style="margin: 0 0 8px; font-size: 24px; font-weight: 800; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.2;">US Q3 2026 GDP Advance Estimate +3.4% — beats ~3.2% est, nearly doubles Q2's pace</h2>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">US Q3 GDP prints +3.4% annualized — above estimate and almost double Q2's +2.2% — with the PCE price index sharply cooler at +2.1%.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
The Bureau of Economic Analysis released the Q3 2026 advance GDP estimate on Thursday October 29 at 8:30 ET: real GDP rose at +3.4% annualized, well above the ~3.2% consensus and up from Q2's final +2.2%. The release landed one day after the FOMC held rates at 3.75–4.00% in a hawkish 10–2 vote.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
Growth was broad-based: consumer spending (health care, food services), business investment (equipment), and government spending all contributed. The quarterly PCE price index decelerated sharply to +2.1% (from Q2's +5.0%) and core PCE fell to +2.5%; real final sales to private domestic purchasers — the cleanest demand signal — hit +4.9%.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
A hotter-than-expected growth print with sharply cooler Q3 price indexes sends a mixed signal to the Fed: strong growth argues against easing, but the improved inflation trajectory supports December rate cuts. The 10Y yield slipped to ~4.99% late Thursday as the GDP beat met risk-off flows.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>
Macro traders and rates desks monitoring the Fed's December meeting playbook after the FOMC's hawkish-hold.
</p>

<h2 style="margin: 0 0 8px; font-size: 24px; font-weight: 800; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.2;">US September PCE: Core +0.3% M/M (below +0.4% est) but headline +3.6% Y/Y — Fed's gauge still sticky above 3%</h2>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">September PCE core cools to +0.3% M/M — just under consensus — but headline runs +0.5% M/M as the Fed's preferred gauge stays stuck above 3% for a fifth straight month.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
The BEA's September Personal Income and Outlays report, released October 29 alongside the Q3 GDP advance estimate, showed headline PCE +0.5% M/M and +3.6% Y/Y. Core PCE (the Fed's preferred gauge) rose +0.3% monthly — just below the +0.4% consensus — and +3.1% Y/Y.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
Personal spending jumped +0.5% nominally but real PCE barely moved (&lt;+0.1%), meaning the gain was nearly all prices rather than volume. Personal income rose +0.2% and the saving rate held at 3.8%, pointing to a consumer running lean.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
The cooler core M/M print was the sole win for doves, but headline PCE at +3.6% Y/Y for a fifth straight month reinforces the FOMC's hawkish-hold stance. Two Fed speakers on Thursday called for higher rates, and December rate-cut odds slipped further.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>
Macro traders, rates desks, and Fed-watchers building their December FOMC positioning.
</p>
