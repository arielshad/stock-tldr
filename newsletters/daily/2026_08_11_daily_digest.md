<h2 style="margin: 0 0 8px; font-size: 24px; font-weight: 800; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.2;">Nvidia + Apollo, Blackstone, BlackRock, Brookfield, Goldman & KKR mobilize >$500B for AI infrastructure — NVDA -~3%, ~$130B market cap lost</h2>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">Six of Wall Street's biggest asset managers will build a >$500B financing rail for AI infrastructure buyers of Nvidia hardware.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
Nvidia is partnering with Apollo, Blackstone, BlackRock (GIP), Brookfield, Goldman Sachs and KKR to mobilize >$500B of third-party capital for hyperscalers, frontier AI labs and enterprises to fund data centers, power and Nvidia hardware. Jensen Huang told CNBC he approached all six firms and none turned him down.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
The consortium creates dedicated financing pools "at significant scale at attractive rates" for Nvidia customers, extending prior AI-financing programs (e.g. the June $35B Apollo/Blackstone–Broadcom deal) into a Nvidia-anchored rail covering chips, energy and real assets.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
It formalizes private-credit/infra funds as the financing layer between Nvidia and its buyers — letting hyperscalers and enterprises fund GPUs and data centers off balance sheet. NVDA fell ~3% and shed ~$130B in market cap Monday as the market read the arrangement as a capex-demand hedge rather than demand pull.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>
AI-infrastructure allocators, private-credit desks, mega-cap semi investors.
</p>

<h2 style="margin: 0 0 8px; font-size: 24px; font-weight: 800; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.2;">Oil +~5% Monday — Brent settles ~$87.72, WTI ~$82.13 as Iran's FM demands sanctions relief and "war reparations" before Hormuz reopens</h2>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">Brent +5% to ~$87.72 Monday as the Iran/Oman handshake to reopen the Strait of Hormuz slips past its expected date.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
The Iran/Oman diplomatic track that had dragged Brent below $82 last week collapsed over the weekend. Iranian FM Abbas Araghchi said the Strait of Hormuz will not reopen "until the United States corrects its behaviour" — explicitly demanding sanctions relief and "war reparations" first.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
Only 8–15 vessels crossed the strait Aug 4–6, vs ~130/day pre-war (roughly one-fifth of global oil supply). Iran's Supreme Leader also reshuffled senior military ranks Sunday, naming a new armed-forces chief of staff and IRGC commander.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
Higher crude reprices Fed-cut math: the July CPI print due Wed Aug 12 now faces an energy headwind, giving hawks an offset argument for keeping September live even after last Friday's -23K NFP miss. The Energy Select Sector ETF (XLE) still closed -1.2% — a supply-shock read, not a demand rally.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>
Energy desks, macro allocators, EM FX traders.
</p>

<h2 style="margin: 0 0 8px; font-size: 24px; font-weight: 800; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.2;">Intel (INTC) launches proposed $15B underwritten common stock offering — JPM, Goldman, MS, Citi lead; $2.25B greenshoe; INTC closes $101.65 after -3.7% dip</h2>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">Intel taps public equity for a $15B common raise on Monday Aug 10 to fund its foundry and AI capex push.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
Intel priced a proposed $15B underwritten public offering of common stock Monday Aug 10, led by JPMorgan, Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley and Citi, with a 30-day greenshoe of up to $2.25B (max deal size $17.25B). Proceeds are earmarked for capital expenditures and working capital.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
The offering is common stock — not preferred or convertible — meaning immediate dilution rather than a debt overhang. Intel says the raise is "intended to further enable Intel to pursue growth opportunities ahead while maintaining a strong balance sheet and its commitment to an investment-grade rating."
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
It's the largest US common-equity raise of the year, and marks Intel choosing the dilution route over issuing more debt to fund its foundry/AI-capex plan. Shares fell to an intraday low of $98.03 (-~3.7%) before recovering to $101.65 as buyers treated the capital cushion as a balance-sheet de-risking event.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>
Semis investors, foundry watchers, ECM desks.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">The July payrolls miss + a soft dollar + light CTA positioning triggered silver's biggest single-day rally in weeks.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
Spot silver rallied 4.9% (+$3.00) to $64.38/oz on Friday Aug 7 to a six-week high after U.S. nonfarm payrolls fell 23K in July versus consensus of +80K (May+June combined revisions also -103K). Gold added 1.8% to ~$4,341.94/oz; iShares Silver Trust (SLV) closed +2.2% at $57.09.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
Goldman noted Managed Money silver net length was at just a 3% two-year rank on July 28 — extremely light positioning. Once the NFP print hit, CTAs covered shorts and front-ran the trend, layered on top of a 1.7% DXY drop (July 28–Aug 5) and ongoing London physical demand.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
