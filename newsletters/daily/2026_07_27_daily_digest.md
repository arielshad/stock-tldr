<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">Oil crashes ~5% Sunday as U.S. and Iran hit pause on strikes for a second straight day and Trump 'gives talks some space'.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>

</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
Waltz told NBC's "Meet the Press" that talks are ongoing "at every level" while adding that "additional military assets are moving into the region." Iranian and Omani officials have been holding what Tehran calls constructive talks.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
Brent front-month fell $4.89 (-5.05%) to $91.89 and WTI dropped $4.67 (-5.23%) to $84.64, both at their lowest in nearly a week, unwinding part of the surge that briefly took Brent above $100. Fewer than 10 commodity vessels a day transited Hormuz over the weekend, so any restoration of flows is expected to be gradual.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>
Energy desks, macro traders, and tanker names watching whether the ceasefire pause holds into Monday's U.S. open.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">New Section 301 duties on 60 trading partners replace the expiring 10% global baseline — effective 12:01 a.m. ET Friday, covering ~99% of U.S. imports.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
The Trump administration announced tariffs of 10% to 12.5% on 60 U.S. trading partners it accuses of failing to crack down on forced labor in supply chains, imposed under Section 301 of U.S. trade law and effective at 12:01 a.m. ET Friday July 24 — replacing the flat 10% global baseline that expired the same morning.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
Most countries, including China and Vietnam, face a 12.5% rate; a lower 10% applies to 17 countries with existing forced-labor prohibitions such as the UK, Canada, and Mexico. Exemptions cover oil and gas, most USMCA-compliant goods, and sectors already under dedicated tariffs (steel, autos).
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
The step-up to 12.5% hits import prices right as Brent closes above $100 — a two-way squeeze on CPI heading into next Wednesday's July 29 FOMC decision. Most affected partners rejected the forced-labor framing but signaled negotiation over retaliation, at least for now.
</p>

<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>
Trade desks, macro strategists, importers, and apparel and consumer-discretionary analysts tracking CPI pass-through ahead of the July 29 FOMC.
