<h2 style="margin: 0 0 8px; font-size: 24px; font-weight: 800; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.2;">Constellation Energy (CEG) +~15% to ~$307 — Google signs 20-year PPA for 890 MW of new nuclear capacity + 15-year 2.7 GW supply deal; $4.3B investment</h2>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">Google backstops 890 MW of new nuclear uprates with a 20-year PPA, and separately locks in 2.7 GW of firm PJM supply for 15 years.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
Alphabet's Google and Constellation Energy announced a landmark clean-energy deal on Tuesday Oct 6, 2026. Google will offtake 890 MW of new nuclear capacity unlocked by equipment upgrades at 11 existing Constellation-owned reactors in Illinois, Pennsylvania and New Jersey, delivered into the PJM grid under a 20-year PPA.
</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
The 890 MW comes from nuclear uprates — modernised turbines, steam generators and digital controls that lift efficiency at existing reactors rather than building new ones. Constellation commits ~$4.3B of capex with first deliveries expected in 2028; Google also takes a 15-year 2,700 MW supply agreement from Constellation's broader PJM fleet.
</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
This is the largest commercial nuclear uprate PPA ever signed by a US hyperscaler and reinforces the thesis that AI data-center load is turning existing nuclear fleets into long-dated infrastructure assets. CEG closed +~15% to ~$306.98, dragging other nuclear and SMR names higher across the sector.
</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>
AI-infra investors, power &amp; utilities desks, and nuclear supply chain traders watching the hyperscaler energy build-out.
</p>

<h2 style="margin: 0 0 8px; font-size: 24px; font-weight: 800; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.2;">US September 2026 ISM Services PMI 54.9 vs 55.0 est — 27th straight expansion; Employment back above 50, Prices climb to 74.0</h2>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">Services PMI 54.9 lands in line — Business Activity cools 5.2 pts but Employment flips back to expansion and Prices stay hot at 74.0.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
The ISM Services PMI for September 2026, released Oct 5 at 10:00 ET, printed 54.9 — essentially in line with the 55.0 consensus and down 0.5 pts from August's 55.4. It is the 27th consecutive month of services expansion.
</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
Business Activity dropped 5.2 points to 56.5 (from Aug's 61.7, the highest since Nov 2022), while Employment rebounded 2.3 points to 50.1 — its first expansion read in three months. Prices Paid rose 1.4 points to 74.0, with its 12-month average at the highest since March 2023.
</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
Services growth is holding up after Friday's weak NFP (+29K vs +84K est), but Prices at 74.0 is not consistent with 2% inflation. Combined with hot ISM Mfg Prices and Core PCE +3.0% Y/Y, it reinforces the stagflation framing that has collapsed Oct Fed hike odds to ~20% from 64% a week ago.
</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>
Macro traders, rates desks, and Fed watchers ahead of the Sept FOMC minutes (Oct 8) and Sept CPI (Oct 15).
