<h2 style="margin: 0 0 8px; font-size: 24px; font-weight: 800; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.2;">10Y Treasury yield tops 5.22% — highest since 2007 — as NY Fed's Williams calls a 2026 hike "reasonable"</h2>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">Williams pivots from 'wait and see' to 'reasonable to hike again this year' — 10Y spikes to a 19-year high and rate-hike bets surge.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
NY Fed President John Williams told the London Macro Policy Forum on Thursday that investor pricing implying another rate hike this year "seems to me a reasonable way of thinking about it," citing inflation still above target, high energy prices, and AI-capex-driven demand. It marks a hawkish pivot from his Sept 2 "wait and see" framing.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
The 10Y Treasury yield spiked to 5.225% intraday — the highest since July 2007 — before settling near 5.15%. The 30Y hit its highest since 2004. Fed-funds-futures-implied odds of an October 28 hike jumped to 77.5% from 53% the day before Williams spoke. The MOVE index (bond-vol gauge) hit 104, its highest since March.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
Williams is a permanent FOMC voter and traditionally the number-two voice at the Fed. His pivot removes the last dovish anchor and validates the Sept–Oct rate-hike repricing that has moved yields ~50bp in three weeks. The 5.22% level is territory the S&P 500 has not had to digest since before the GFC.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>
Rates traders, fixed-income desks, equity duration investors watching the discount-rate repricing.
</p>

<h2 style="margin: 0 0 8px; font-size: 24px; font-weight: 800; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.2;">Bitget confirms $387.5M hot-wallet hack — largest crypto exchange theft of 2026 — CEO names North Korea suspects</h2>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">Attackers spoofed transaction data through a compromised Bitget backend to drain $387.5M — the biggest crypto exchange heist of 2026.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
Bitget detected unauthorized transfers from its hot and warm wallets at 18:31 UTC on Thursday Sept 24. The exchange initially pegged the loss at $351.6M; by Friday CEO Gracy Chen confirmed $387.5M after Zcash and TRON balances were added — the largest single CEX theft of 2026.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
Attackers compromised a backend system inside Bitget's wallet infrastructure and spoofed transaction data to trigger the exchange's own authorization process — private keys were not stolen and cold wallets were untouched. Funds moved across seven chains. Bitget says the full loss is covered by its ~$464M User Protection Fund. BTC withdrawals restart Sept 28 08:00 UTC; ETH Sept 29, USDT Sept 30.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
TRM Labs and CEO Chen both point to indicators consistent with North Korean actors — VPN IPs linked to DPRK crews and laundering wallets that overlap with prior Bybit and AFX Bridge hits. BGB fell ~4.6%; BTC and ETH each held within ±0.3%, suggesting muted broader market impact — but the incident refocuses attention on the safety of centralized custody.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>
Crypto desks, exchange risk teams, on-chain analysts tracking DPRK laundering patterns.
</p>

<h2 style="margin: 0 0 8px; font-size: 24px; font-weight: 800; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.2;">Nike (NKE) — BofA's Hutchinson cuts to Underperform, $30 PT (from $47); FY27 EPS estimate cut 11% ahead of Oct 1 earnings</h2>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">BofA rips Nike PT to $30, tags it Underperform ahead of Oct 1 earnings — dividend flagged as at-risk on cut EPS.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
Bank of America analyst Lorraine Hutchinson cut Nike from Neutral to Underperform on Sept 25, dropping her price target to $30 from $47 — roughly 17% below Nike's $35.75 close. The call landed six trading days before Nike is due to report Q1 fiscal-2027 earnings on Oct 1.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
Hutchinson lowered her FY27 EPS estimate 11% to $1.43 and her FY28 estimate 12% to $1.87, putting her FY27 view about 14% below consensus. She cites weak wholesale momentum in North America and continued sales pressure in Greater China. The $1.64 annual dividend now exceeds her FY27 EPS estimate — flagging it as at-risk.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
Nike is the largest holding in most large-cap apparel and consumer-discretionary baskets, so a Street underperform + $30 tape sets the bar for the coming print. Peers moved on the read-through: LULU -0.34%, UAA -1.56%. Traders now key on whether the Oct 1 print resets FY27 guidance far enough to protect the dividend.
</p>

<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>
Consumer-discretionary desks, dividend investors, and anyone positioned ahead of Nike's Oct 1 earnings print.
