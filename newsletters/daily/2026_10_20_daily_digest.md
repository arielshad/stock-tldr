<h2 style="margin: 0 0 8px; font-size: 24px; font-weight: 800; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.2;">China Q3 2026 GDP +4.5% Y/Y — misses ~4.8% consensus; retail sales +1.1%, property investment -20.6% YTD</h2>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">China Q3 2026 GDP +4.5% Y/Y misses ~4.8% MNI preview as property investment dives 20.6% YTD and retail sales slow to +1.1%.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
China's National Bureau of Statistics released Q3 2026 GDP at +4.5% Y/Y, decelerating from Q2's +4.3% advance. Jan-Sep cumulative GDP totaled CNY 107.1 trillion (+4.6% Y/Y), tracking below Beijing's unofficial "around 5%" full-year target.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
Jan-Sep fixed-asset investment fell 7.2% Y/Y, with real-estate development investment down 20.6%. September retail sales grew just 1.1% Y/Y while industrial value-added rose a healthier 5.5% — the factory sector carrying what consumers cannot.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
The two-speed economy sharpens: exports and factory output are running hot while domestic demand stalls near zero real growth. A 20.6% YTD contraction in real-estate development investment keeps the balance-sheet recession structural, not cyclical.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>
Macro traders, China/EM funds, commodity desks — China proxies FXI, MCHI, KWEB, iron-ore futures and base metals all react to this read.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">Cleveland-Cliffs posts its best quarter in three years — adj EBITDA $612M beats its own $575M guide by 6%.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
Cleveland-Cliffs, the largest US flat-rolled steel producer and top iron ore pellet supplier, reported Q3 2026 results before the Mon Oct 19 open. Revenue was $5.6B on 4.2 million net tons of external steelmaking shipments, with GAAP EPS swinging to $0.26 from -$0.51 a year ago.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
Adj EBITDA of $612M jumped $326M from Q2's $286M, beating management's own ~$575M guide. For Q4 2026, CLF guides adj EBITDA to ~$715M even with seasonal maintenance outages, and CEO Lourenco Goncalves sees >$4B for full-year 2027 based on signed contracts.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
CLF is the US steel bellwether, so a sharp Q3 EBITDA ramp signals that domestic HRC pricing and auto-grade shipments stabilized after a weak H1. The $715M Q4 guide and >$4B 2027 outlook anchor the whole domestic steel group — NUE, STLD, X — heading into their own prints.
</p>

<h2 style="margin: 0 0 8px; font-size: 24px; font-weight: 800; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.2;">Trump EO waives nearly all Graham Act Russia sanctions — up-to-500% tariffs, secondary bank sanctions, sovereign-debt bans all shelved</h2>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">Trump signs EO Fri Oct 16 waiving nearly all of the new Lindsey O. Graham Russia-Iran sanctions act two days before it was due to kick in.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
President Trump signed an executive order late Friday Oct 16 waiving nearly all substantive sanctions, tariffs and restrictions in the Lindsey O. Graham Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act — the same law he signed only a month earlier on Sept 18. The waiver came two days before the Russia provisions were set to take effect on Oct 18.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
The Graham Act — passed 86-11 in the Senate and 262-159 in the House — imposed tariffs of up to 500% on Russian goods, up to 100% on the top five buyers of Russian crude and gas, secondary sanctions on foreign banks, and bans on U.S. purchases of Russian sovereign debt. The EO also waives the provision requiring existing sanctions to stay in place.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
This is the second Russia sanctions waiver in two weeks — the Oct 9 OFAC GL-135 waived Russia diesel sanctions through April 2027. Bipartisan Senate backlash was immediate: Cruz, Haley, Tillis and Cornyn all broke ranks; Zelenskyy said it "definitely helps only the Russians." Energy and FX markets reset the Russia risk premium.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>
Energy desks, geopolitical risk analysts, and FX traders watching Urals crude, ruble and the broader EM commodity risk premium.
</p>
