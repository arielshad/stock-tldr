<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">Costco's Q4 tops estimates on $95.7B revenue and 9.4% comps, but a $0.15 tariff-refund tailwind flatters EPS and shares only tick up 0.26% after hours.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
Costco reported Q4 and full-year fiscal 2026 results after the close Thursday, September 24. Consensus had modeled $94.86B revenue and $6.54 EPS for the 16-week quarter ended August 30.
</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
Q4 revenue $95.72B (+11.1% Y/Y), diluted EPS $6.75 including a non-recurring $0.15/sh IEEPA tariff-refund benefit. Total company comps +9.4% (+6.7% ex fuel/FX); membership fee income $1.85B (+7.3%), 150.4M cardholders at 89.8% renewal. FY26: $303.15B total revenue, $20.76 diluted EPS, 939 warehouses (25 net new).
</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
COST is one of two mega-cap defensives (WMT the other) that price in U.S. consumer resilience. The muted +0.26% AH reaction shows the tariff-refund flattered a beat the market largely priced in — the 6.7% comp ex fuel/FX confirms trade-down demand but the bar was already high after Q3's +11.6% top line.
</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>
Consumer/staples analysts and factor allocators long quality-defensives watching October monthly sales and the November MFI-hike anniversary.

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">Oracle sends a force majeure notice to Blue Owl's Stack Infrastructure over Project Jupiter after NM regulators twice deny the pipeline that feeds the campus.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
Oracle told Blue Owl-controlled Stack Infrastructure that circumstances beyond its control may prevent Project Jupiter — the 2.45-GW, $165B New Mexico data center anchoring the Stargate AI buildout — from coming online by its 2028 deadline. The notice gives Oracle legal room to defer lease payments if the buildout slips.
</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
New Mexico's State Land Office twice denied Energy Transfer's 17-mile gas pipeline (March and July), pushing the in-service date to Feb 1, 2027. Bloom Energy's solid-oxide fuel cells — the campus's power spine — can't run without that gas. About 20 banks lent ~$18B to build the campus; that debt is now marked below 90 cents on the dollar.
</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
This is the first crack in the Stargate story — a top-3 hyperscaler is publicly hedging its exposure to an AI-datacenter mega-project. Blue Owl (OWL) fell as the debt gapped down; Bloom Energy (BE) traded off with fuel-cell demand in doubt.
</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>
AI-capex traders, private-credit and BDC desks, power-and-infrastructure analysts watching the Energy Transfer permit and any Blue Owl 8-K response.

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">FDA advisory panel backs Grail's Galleri multi-cancer blood test 7-2 on benefit-risk — the first federal endorsement of a multi-cancer early-detection screen — as the final FDA decision looms.</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
The FDA's Molecular and Clinical Genetics Devices Panel met Wednesday September 23 to review GRAIL's PMA for Galleri, a blood test that scans a single draw for DNA fragments shed by ~50 cancers. About 117,000 people paid out-of-pocket (~$949 per test) for Galleri in H1 2026.
</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
The panel voted 10-0 on safety, 6-4 on effectiveness, and 7-2 (1 abstention) that benefits outweigh risks. GRAL had ripped +36% cumulatively into the meeting, then closed -3.23% to $105.01 Thursday on the classic "sell-the-panel" fade. Recommendations are non-binding but the FDA typically follows panels.
</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
This is the first advisory-committee endorsement of any multi-cancer early-detection test — the entire MCED category (EXAS, NVTA, ILMN-adjacent) has been waiting for a regulatory template. A PMA grant opens the door to CMS reimbursement conversations; the 6-4 effectiveness split telegraphs tight labeling and post-market study requirements.
</p>
<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>
Diagnostics analysts, biotech long/short, and healthcare policy desks watching for the final FDA PMA decision and any reimbursement statements from CMS or private payers.
</p>
