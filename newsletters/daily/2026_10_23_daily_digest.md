<h2 style="margin: 0 0 8px; font-size: 24px; font-weight: 800; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.2;">Nasdaq -2.6% — AI selloff + oil spike collide: Meta -7%, Intel -8%, CoreWeave -10%; WTI tops $100, VIX +14%</h2>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">Tech stocks got smacked as AI-spending fears collided with an oil spike on fresh Iran-war worries.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
Thursday Oct 22 was a split-tape risk-off session: the Nasdaq Composite fell 2.6% to 26,008.86 and the S&P 500 shed 1.2% to 7,663.43, driven simultaneously by an AI-capex reality check on mega-cap names after Meta and Tesla's Wednesday-night reports and a jump in oil on fresh geopolitical risk.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
Meta closed -7% at $648.85 after a $9.89B teen-safety charge and an aggressive $135–145B FY26 capex guide; CoreWeave fell ~10%, Intel -8%, Lam Research -9%, and Nvidia -4.4%. The New York Times reported the U.S. and Israel had drawn up plans for strikes on Iran after the Nov 3 midterms, pushing Brent past $112 and WTI to ~$100 — a 5-month high — while VIX jumped 14% to 19.03.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
This is the first clear read on whether mega-cap AI prints will keep carrying the tape or trigger a vol regime change — hyperscaler capex is accelerating but the market is now charging a higher cost of capital for names debt-financing the build-out. Overlay a renewed Iran-war crude premium and the macro backdrop stops being a one-way street.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>
Tech/semi traders, macro desks, and energy investors heading into next week's AAPL, MSFT, GOOGL, and AMZN prints.
</p>

<h2 style="margin: 0 0 8px; font-size: 24px; font-weight: 800; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.2;">Western Digital (WDC) Q1 FY27 — revenue $4.38B (+55% Y/Y) smashes guide; Q2 guided to $5.1B (+66–72% Y/Y); dividend raised 17%</h2>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">AI-storage demand pushed WDC's quarter well above its own prior guide, and the Q2 outlook is a bigger step-up.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
Western Digital reported fiscal Q1 2027 after the close on Thursday Oct 22: $4.38B of revenue (+55% Y/Y, +17% Q/Q), above the top of its $4.0–$4.2B August guide. Non-GAAP diluted EPS of $4.64 crushed the ~$4.07 consensus.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
Non-GAAP gross margin was 58.1% and operating margin 49.1% — records tied to tight hyperscale nearline HDD supply. Free cash flow was $1.38B and the quarterly dividend was raised 17% to $0.175/sh. Q2 FY27 guide: revenue $5.1B ±$100M (+66–72% Y/Y), EPS $5.80 ±$0.20, gross margin 60–61%.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
WDC is one of the only pure-plays on nearline HDD economics after the SanDisk NAND split. The Q2 revenue step from $4.38B to a $5.1B midpoint — and GM from 58% to 60–61% — is the company's biggest sequential guide ever, landing into a session where AI-infra peers were selling off on cost-of-capital fears.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>
AI-infra traders and semis desks watching read-through to Seagate (STX), Marvell (MRVL), and the broader AI-infrastructure complex.
</p>
