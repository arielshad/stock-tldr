<h2 style="margin: 0 0 8px; font-size: 24px; font-weight: 800; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.2;">US-Canada trade talks collapse — 50% Section 338 tariffs on ~$20B of Canadian goods effective at midnight Aug 22, Carney vows dollar-for-dollar retaliation</h2>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">First-ever Section 338 tariffs — 50% duties on ~$20B of Canadian goods live after Friday-night talks collapse.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
US-Canada trade negotiations collapsed shortly before a midnight Friday deadline, sending previously-paused Section 338 tariffs live at 12:01 a.m. ET on Saturday, Aug 22 2026. The duties add a 50% surcharge on roughly $20 billion of Canadian imports — including cement, softwood lumber, hockey sticks, certain building materials, and liquors.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
The 50% duty is imposed under Section 338 of the Tariff Act of 1930 — a statute never before invoked by a president since it became law. Trump had originally proclaimed the tariffs to take effect Aug 19, then paused them 3 days as negotiators worked; US Trade Representative Jamieson Greer cited "new demands and walk backs" by Canada as the deal-breaker.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
First presidential use of Section 338 opens a new legal channel for tariff action after the Supreme Court struck down the earlier IEEPA-based duties in February. Canadian retaliation on the same $20B scale escalates the trade war heading into Jackson Hole — watch autos, homebuilders, softwood lumber names, and USD/CAD.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>
Macro traders, autos and homebuilder desks, USD/CAD FX.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">US private-sector activity hit its fastest pace since April 2022 in August, all on the services side.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>

Services (56.8) was well above the 54.0 consensus while manufacturing (53.2) missed 53.9 and cooled to a 13-month low.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
PMI readings above 50 signal expansion. Services ran at its fastest pace since December 2024 while goods production posted its weakest increase in 13 months, partly on lengthening supply-chain delays. Employment grew at the fastest pace since early 2025 and business expectations for the next 12 months hit a nine-month high.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>

A strong growth signal five days before Chair Warsh's Jackson Hole keynote reduces the odds of near-term Fed easing — 10Y yields ticked higher on the print while equities held (S&amp;P 500 +0.43%).

<h2 style="margin: 0 0 8px; font-size: 24px; font-weight: 800; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.2;">Alibaba (BABA) Q1 FY27 — revenue RMB 269B (+9%) beats but net income -75% as AI capex hits ~$10B (+75% Y/Y); Cloud +45% (22-quarter high); ADRs -~4%</h2>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">Alibaba's June-quarter profit crashes 75% as AI capex more than doubles, but Cloud growth and revenue beat make the bull case for the AI pivot.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
Alibaba reported fiscal Q1 2027 (quarter ended June 30) revenue of RMB 268.95B (~$39.64B, +9% Y/Y), above the $38.63B estimate. GAAP net income fell 75% to RMB 10.54B as capital spending exploded to ~$9.98B (+75% Y/Y) on AI infrastructure. Adjusted EPS per ADS was $1.26 vs $1.85 est; ADRs fell ~4%.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
External Alibaba Cloud revenue jumped 45% — its fastest in 22 quarters — and AI-related product revenue hit its 12th straight quarter of triple-digit Y/Y growth. Free cash flow was a -RMB 44.67B outflow. Chairman Joe Tsai defended the spend, arguing compute shortages persist through 2030 with three-year payback periods.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
This is the cleanest test yet of whether Chinese hyperscalers can convert AI capex into cash the way US mega-caps have. Bulls point to the 45% Cloud print and the 12-quarter AI-product streak; bears point at negative FCF and the shrinking core-commerce cash cow. Read-throughs to BIDU, Tencent, PDD, and US AI-infra names.
