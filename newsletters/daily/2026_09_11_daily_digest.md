<h2 style="margin: 0 0 8px; font-size: 24px; font-weight: 800; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.2;">Oracle Q1 FY27 — cloud infra +121%, RPO $664B (+$209B Y/Y), FY27 guide raised to $90B+</h2>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">Oracle's Q1 FY27 lands the biggest RPO print in enterprise software history and raises FY27 revenue guide to at least $90B.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
Oracle reported fiscal Q1 2027 after the bell Thursday Sept 10. Total revenue $19.3B (+30% Y/Y) and non-GAAP EPS $1.92 both beat, but the number that matters is remaining performance obligations (RPO): $664B — up $209B year-over-year and $26B sequentially, blowing past the $630.6B consensus.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
Cloud infrastructure revenue hit $7.4B (+121% Y/Y), with CPU/GPU infra alone at $6.5B (+151%). Oracle booked more than $30B in new AI cloud contracts in Q1 alone, and FY27 capex is guided at $90–95B with GPU utilization running at 97.9%.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
A $209B Y/Y RPO jump means enterprise buyers are locking in Oracle capacity years out — effectively signing the demand curve for AI infra. Reads through to NVDA/AMD/AVGO demand and settles the hyperscaler-capex debate for now. ORCL rebounded +4.34% after hours to $159.58 after falling -5.38% intraday.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>
AI-infra and cloud allocators watching the real demand signal behind hyperscaler capex.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">Saudi August production plunges 1.9 mbd MoM to 6.238 mbd — the lowest since the Gulf War — as US–Iran hostilities choke export routes; Brent settles above $105.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
Saudi Arabia notified OPEC that August 2026 crude production fell 1.9 million barrels per day to 6.238 mbd — the lowest reported figure since 1990. The kingdom cited renewed US–Iran hostilities and continuing Houthi strikes on Red Sea shipping and southern-Saudi energy sites, not a voluntary policy cut.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
On Thursday Sept 10, Brent (November) settled +3.90% at $105.16 and WTI (October) +4.31% at $100.16. A disappointing Treasury buyback ($6B vs $8–10B expected) compounded the inflation fear, pushing the US 10-year yield to 4.902% — the highest close since October 2023.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
A physical supply loss of ~1.9 mbd from the world's largest swing producer is structural, not a headline scare — spare-capacity math gets tight into Q4 with the SPR at multi-decade lows. Reads through to XLE/OIH/refiners long and to rate-sensitives short heading into Fri CPI and the Sept 15–16 FOMC.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>
Energy desks, macro traders, and rates allocators tracking the inflation-into-Fed nexus.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">Hot August PPI — headline matches est but Y/Y ticks up as diesel jumps 24.1%; keeps Fed hike bet alive into next week's decision.</p>

</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
Final Demand goods jumped +1.1% MoM on energy (+4.2%), with diesel fuel prices alone surging 24.1% — accounting for over one-third of the goods increase. Core PPI (ex food, energy, trade) +0.3% MoM / +4.7% Y/Y, both a tenth above consensus. Services rose only +0.1% MoM.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
The print keeps the Sept 15–16 FOMC contest tight between a hold and a 25bp hike, with 10Y yields already at 4.902% — highest since Oct 2023. August CPI drops today (Fri Sept 11); a matching or hotter print materially raises hike odds. Diesel's spike also feeds into trucking, logistics, and food-processing cost pressure.
</p>
