<h2 style="margin: 0 0 8px; font-size: 24px; font-weight: 800; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.2;">Meta Platforms (META) Q3 2026 — $64.60B Revenue Crushes Estimates, Q4 Guide Tops Street</h2>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">Meta prints a $64.6B topline beat but takes a $9.89B teen-safety charge; Q4 guide blows past Street while capex guide narrows up.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
Meta Platforms reported Q3 2026 results after the close on October 21. Revenue of $64.60B grew 26% Y/Y and beat Street at ~$61.3B — GAAP diluted EPS came in at $3.83, dragged down by a $9.89B pre-tax charge for a settlement reached with 52 U.S. state attorneys general over teen mental-health litigation.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
Ex-charge, operating income was $22.88B at 35.4% margin and diluted EPS was $7.40. Reality Labs posted a $4.13B operating loss on $443M revenue. Q4 revenue guidance of $73.0–$76.0B tops ~$70B consensus, and FY26 capex was narrowed to $135–$145B from $130–$145B.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
Revenue acceleration + the Q4 guide confirm Meta's AI ad-targeting bull case is intact. The $9.89B charge is the biggest single-quarter legal hit in Meta's history, with the full $18.59B liability spread over multiple years. The narrowed, higher capex floor signals continued hyperscaler-level spending — read-through for NVDA, AVGO, and GOOGL (reports next week).
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>
Mega-cap tech traders, ad-platform investors, and AI-capex desks tracking the hyperscaler spending cycle.
</p>

</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>

</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
Tesla is now spending hyperscaler-level capex but still earning like an auto OEM — the 322bp op-margin collapse is the story. Musk's cautious commentary on Optimus timing and robotaxi regulation weighed on shares. Tesla is now a top-10 capex buyer alongside the hyperscalers, reinforcing the AI capex floor thesis.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">TXN delivers the clearest analog-cycle beat in years — 28% Y/Y revenue, 77% EPS growth, 47.5% op margin.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
Texas Instruments reported Q3 2026 after the close October 21. Revenue of $6.055B grew 28% Y/Y and beat the ~$5.92B consensus; GAAP diluted EPS was $2.62, up 77% Y/Y, well above the ~$2.42 estimate — beating the high end of the company's own July guidance.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
Analog, the core franchise, delivered $4.91B revenue (+32% Y/Y) and $2.51B operating profit (+69%), operating at 51% segment margins. Q3 FCF was $2.96B and TTM FCF reached $8.42B — 40.6% of TTM revenue. Q4 guidance: revenue $5.80B–$6.30B, EPS $2.45–$2.79.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
This is the clearest analog cycle turn in years — Analog growing 32% Y/Y confirms broad industrial, auto, and data-center demand, not just AI. Read-through is bullish for ADI, MCHP, NXPI, ON Semi, and STM. TXN also validated its countercyclical capex plan: FCF is back above 40% of revenue.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>
Analog, industrial, and auto-semis desks; anyone tracking the broader semiconductor up-cycle.
</p>

<h2 style="margin: 0 0 8px; font-size: 24px; font-weight: 800; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.2;">AT&T (T) Q3 2026 — Beat and Raise, but $2.6B Mexico Impairment and Higher Capex Sink Stock -6%</h2>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">AT&T beats on top and bottom line and raises FY26 EPS, but a $2.6B Mexico impairment and heavy capex plan sink the stock ~6%.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
AT&T reported Q3 2026 results before the open October 21. Revenue grew 5.3% to $32.3B, the best consolidated service-revenue growth in more than four years; adjusted EPS of $0.65 beat consensus around $0.62. GAAP EPS fell to $0.13 due to a $2.6B non-cash impairment on its held-for-sale Mexico wireless business.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
AT&T delivered a record 769K Advanced Connectivity internet net adds (442K fiber + 327K FWA) and 379K postpaid phone net adds. FCF was $5.0B. Management raised FY26 adj EPS to the ~$2.40 range and projected ~$10B of share repurchases in 2026, while lifting capital investment to $23–$24B.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
The headline beat and raise is overshadowed by the Mexico impairment hitting GAAP EPS and by the higher capex envelope needed to hit the 60M+ fiber-locations-by-2030 target. Shares fell ~6.4% to $21.92, dragging telecom peers VZ and TMUS as investors reassess AT&T's cash return pace against its ongoing network build.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>
Telecom equity holders, dividend investors, and credit desks tracking telco capex and buyback pacing.
</p>
