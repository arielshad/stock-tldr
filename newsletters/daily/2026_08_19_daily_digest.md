<h2 style="margin: 0 0 8px; font-size: 24px; font-weight: 800; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.2;">Klarna (KLAR) Q2 2026 — revenue $1.04B (+27% Y/Y) tops est but shares plunge ~19% as FY26 GMV guide cut to $149–151B on German consumer weakness</h2>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">Klarna beat Q2 but cut its full-year volume and revenue outlook, and the stock got hammered on the German consumer read-through.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
Buy-now-pay-later fintech Klarna Group (NYSE: KLAR) reported Q2 2026 results before the U.S. open on Aug 18. Q2 came in above consensus on both revenue and profitability, but the company trimmed its 2026 volume and revenue outlook, citing weaker consumer discretionary spending in Germany, its largest market by GMV.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
Q2 revenue was $1.04B (+27% Y/Y) vs $996M est; adj EPS $0.01 vs consensus -$0.06. For the full year, Klarna cut its GMV outlook to $149–151B (from >$155B) and revenue to $4.08–4.16B (from >$4.34B), blaming ~$600M on FX plus softer European volumes concentrated in Germany. Management raised transaction-margin-dollar guidance to $1.62–1.65B — framed as "less business at better economics."
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
Germany is the euro area's largest economy and a bellwether for European discretionary spend. The guide cut revives KLAR's post-IPO profitability-vs-growth debate and keeps the stock below its September 2025 $40 IPO price. Read-across into other BNPL and euro-consumer names is cautiously negative.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>
Fintech investors, European macro desks, and consumer-discretionary analysts tracking BNPL demand signals across the eurozone.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">This year's best-performing corner of Wall Street got pummeled, dragging the Nasdaq down 1.3% and reversing Monday's memory-chip rally.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
US semiconductor stocks led a broad risk-off session on Tuesday Aug 18. The PHLX Semiconductor Index (SOX) fell about 5% — its worst session in roughly two weeks — sending the Nasdaq Composite down 1.3% and the S&P 500 down 0.6%. The neocloud infrastructure cohort (CoreWeave, Nebius, TeraWulf) fell 8–12%.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
Individual names: Micron ~-7%, Intel ~-6%, AMD ~-3%, Nvidia ~-2%. The 10-year yield sat around 4.70% and the 30-year around 5.29%. WTI crude rose to ~$84.55/bbl as President Trump reiterated that no U.S.-Iran talks are scheduled, feeding an oil-and-yields headwind for long-duration growth names.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
The move is the mirror image of Monday's memory rally — investors reassessed whether the elevated-yield, elevated-oil backdrop can support AI-capex multiples. It matters most for AI-infrastructure exposures: hyperscaler capex, GPU rentals, and the neocloud complex that had led the year's rally.

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>
Semi traders, AI-infrastructure long-onlies, macro-rates desks, and sector-rotation PMs watching the FOMC minutes (Aug 19) and Jackson Hole (Aug 27–29).

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">Home Depot beats on comps and adj EPS, reaffirms FY26 as CFO says the housing market is still "frozen."</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
Home Depot posted Q2 FY26 results before the open on Aug 18. Sales rose to $47.9B and comparable sales flipped back positive at +1.7% (U.S. comps +1.3%). Adjusted EPS came in at $4.92, ahead of the ~$4.73 Street estimate, and the company reaffirmed its full-year guide.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>

FY26 guidance reaffirmed: sales +2.5–4.5%, comps flat to +2.0%, adj EPS growth flat to +4.0% off $14.69 base — now incorporating IEEPA tariff refunds against higher fuel and product-input costs.

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
This is the first read on U.S. discretionary spending after Friday's -0.6% July retail sales print — and it's a positive one. CFO McPhail said HD is "operating in what I call frozen housing market conditions" but adding "we're taking share." Read-through to Lowe's (LOW) Q2 print Wednesday Aug 19.

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>
Consumer-discretionary and housing-cycle traders; Lowe's watchers ahead of LOW's print Wednesday and Walmart Thursday.

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">Baidu's ad business shrinks for a fourth quarter as AI-cloud spend and GPU rentals do the heavy lifting.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>

</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>

</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
The print reinforces the split running through the Chinese internet complex: search-ad monetization is under structural pressure from AI chatbots while cloud/GPU rental is fast-growing but lower-margin. Management said advertising will remain under pressure in H2, mirroring Alibaba's AI-pivot pattern across the sector.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">

China internet analysts, AI-infrastructure investors, and cross-border tech desks tracking the ad-vs-cloud split across the sector.

<h2 style="margin: 0 0 8px; font-size: 24px; font-weight: 800; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.2;">US 30-year Treasury yield tops 5.31% — highest since 2007 as debt-supply, sticky inflation, and AI-capex issuance drive long-end selloff</h2>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">30Y Treasury yield hits 5.31% Monday, highest since 2007, as long-end sells off on deficits, inflation, and AI-capex bond supply.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
The yield on the 30-year US Treasury climbed roughly six basis points on Monday Aug 17 to 5.31%, breaching last month's high and reaching the loftiest since 2007. Long-end government bond yields also rose in Canada and Germany, reflecting a global reset in the price of long-dated debt.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
The move steepens the curve: the 2Y is down ~12bp month-to-date while the 30Y is up 13bp. Drivers: mounting US debt, inflation stuck above the Fed's 2% target for five years, waning real-money demand at the long end, and a fresh ramp of investment-grade issuance to fund AI capex. Barclays' Anshul Pradhan argues "against fading the long-end selloff." The 2007 GFC-era peak of 5.44% is the next test.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
Rising real yields at the long end tighten financial conditions independently of the Fed's short-rate path — negative for long-duration equity, housing via 30Y mortgage rates, and the Treasury's cost of funding a ~$2T deficit. It also complicates near-term rate-cut expectations heading into FOMC minutes and Jackson Hole.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>
Rates traders, macro desks, duration allocators, and anyone modeling mortgage spreads or long-duration equity multiples.
</p>

<h2 style="margin: 0 0 8px; font-size: 24px; font-weight: 800; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.2;">Memory chip stocks rally Aug 17 — Micron (MU) +5.8%, WDC +5.4%, SanDisk +~5% after Commerce Sec Lutnick tells WSJ Trump admin opposes Apple buying Chinese memory (CXMT/YMTC)</h2>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">Lutnick's "not in favor" comment on Apple sourcing DRAM/NAND from CXMT and YMTC hands US memory names a demand tailwind.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
US Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick told the Wall Street Journal that the Trump administration is "not in favor" of Apple purchasing memory chips from Chinese suppliers ChangXin Memory Technologies (CXMT, DRAM) and Yangtze Memory Technologies (YMTC, NAND), where Apple had been testing chips for products sold outside the US.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
There is no formal rule or export order — Lutnick said he told Apple "plainly" — but the signal alone moved the tape. Micron (MU) closed +5.8% near $1,030, Western Digital (WDC) +5.4%, and SanDisk (SNDK) +~5% (was +10% premarket). The read: Apple's memory dollars would flow to Micron, Samsung, and SK hynix instead.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
Apple is a top-3 memory buyer globally — redirecting even a slice of that demand to MU/Samsung/SK hynix meaningfully tightens an already-tight market. The bull case stacks on SanDisk's Investor Day guidance (FY28–30 mid-to-high-teens rev growth) and BofA's Micron US-1 upgrade. Caveat: no formal rule means no legal enforcement.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>
Semis traders, memory investors, and AI-capex desks tracking NAND/DRAM tightness heading into Micron's F4Q print in late September.
</p>
