<h2 style="margin: 0 0 8px; font-size: 24px; font-weight: 800; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.2;">Two VLCCs Hit by Projectiles Exiting the Strait of Hormuz — WTI +2.8% to $88.16</h2>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">Two supertankers hit by projectiles late Monday inside the Strait of Hormuz — WTI +2.8% to $88.16 as the US-Iran fight reignites after the Aug 30 Larak strikes.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
Maritime security consultancy Marisks and UKMTO reported two very-large crude carriers were struck by unknown projectiles as they exited the Persian Gulf through the Strait of Hormuz on Monday night. The Bahri-operated Sidr and the Sinokor-operated Senegal Prosperity were hit northeast/east of Khasab, Oman. No casualties reported.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
Both tankers were on outbound transits — the highest-risk leg for chokepoint attacks. Reports came within minutes of each other, days after US strikes on Iranian rocket launchers on Larak Island on Aug 30. No group claimed responsibility.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
Roughly 20% of global oil demand and one-third of seaborne LNG transits Hormuz. Two mid-transit VLCC strikes materially raise war-risk premiums and could tighten realized barrels if operators pause outbound loadings. The 10Y yield held at 4.77% (19-month high) and risk assets sold off.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>
Energy desks, tanker-rate and P&amp;I insurance traders, macro/geopolitics allocators.

<h2 style="margin: 0 0 8px; font-size: 24px; font-weight: 800; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.2;">US ISM Manufacturing PMI August 2026 = 54.6 — 8th Straight Month of Expansion, Prices Paid Stuck at 71.1</h2>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">ISM Manufacturing cooled a notch to 54.6 in August but stayed in expansion — Prices Paid still 71.1, a red flag for the hawkish rate-hike case Warsh has been telegraphing.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
The ISM's August 2026 Manufacturing PMI printed at 54.6, one full point below July's 55.6 but marking the eighth consecutive month of expansion. Released Tuesday Sept 1 at 10:00 AM ET.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
New Orders decelerated to 53.7 (-3.0), Production held at 58.3, and Employment slipped to 51.2 (-1.6) but stayed above the growth line. The standout: Prices Paid unchanged at 71.1 — historically consistent with core-goods inflation running near 3% Y/Y.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
This is the exact combo the Warsh-led Fed does not want: growth and employment expanding, but input prices sticky in the low-70s. Rate-hike odds for the Sept 16-17 FOMC hit &gt;65% intraday after Warsh's Aug 28 Jackson Hole speech; a 71.1 Prices Paid reading gives the hawks fresh ammunition.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>
Rates and STIR traders, macro discretionary PMs, and industrials allocators watching the Warsh Fed's reaction function.
