<h2 style="margin: 0 0 8px; font-size: 24px; font-weight: 800; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.2;">Warren Buffett becomes Chairman Emeritus of Berkshire Hathaway</h2>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">Warren Buffett ends a 61-year run as Berkshire chairman; his son Howard takes the chair, Greg Abel keeps the CEO seat.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
Warren Buffett, 96, told Berkshire Hathaway shareholders on Friday Sept 18 that he is stepping down as chairman effective immediately and becoming Chairman Emeritus. His son Howard G. Buffett (71) — a Berkshire director for 33 years — takes the chair, while CEO Greg Abel remains in place.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
Buffett told shareholders "Father Time always wins" after 61 years as chairman (since 1970). He remains on the Berkshire board as an adviser; Howard's role as non-executive chair is designed to preserve Berkshire's culture while Abel runs day-to-day capital allocation and operations.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
BRK.B closed +0.11% at $509.77 — a muted reaction, as the market had been pricing the transition since Abel took the CEO role in January. The formal end of Buffett's chairmanship removes the last operational role of the most influential US investor of the past half-century.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>
Long-only equity allocators, Berkshire watchers, and corporate-governance analysts tracking how the post-Buffett era actually unfolds.
</p>

<h2 style="margin: 0 0 8px; font-size: 24px; font-weight: 800; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.2;">Bank of Japan hikes +25bp to 1.25% — highest since 1995 — in split 7-2 vote</h2>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">BOJ lifts policy rate +25bp to 1.25%, highest since 1995 — but split 7-2 vote and dovish dissent send yen lower and Nikkei to a +1.4% rally.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
The Bank of Japan raised its overnight call rate +25bp to 1.25% from 1.00% on Friday Sept 18 — its second hike in three months and the highest policy rate since 1995. The board split 7-2, with Toichiro Asada and Ayano Sato dissenting in favor of holding.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
Governor Ueda framed the move as normalization with no preset pace for further increases. Markets read the two dissents as capping the near-term hawkish trajectory — paradoxically sending USD/JPY above 157 and the Nikkei up +1.38% to 65,018.95 on the weaker-yen exporter tailwind.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
Japan's exit from ultra-low rates is quickening — three months from the June hike vs. six months from March to June. It also intersects US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent's public call for Japan to "do the right thing," locking in a hike that fits Washington's yen-strength agenda after last week's FOMC hike to 3.75–4.00%.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>
Macro traders, FX desks (USD/JPY), JGB watchers, and Japan equities investors tracking the normalization cadence.
</p>

<h2 style="margin: 0 0 8px; font-size: 24px; font-weight: 800; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.2;">SEC grants 5-year Innovation Exemption for tokenized US stock trading — HOOD, COIN, CRCL +3–5%</h2>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">SEC opens a 5-year on-chain lane for tokenized US stocks after Congress's crypto bill stalls.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
Chair Paul Atkins's SEC issued a 5-year conditional exemptive order on Thursday Sept 17 letting Tokenized Securities Venues (TSVs) trade tokenized NMS stocks via permissioned automated market makers without registering as an exchange — two days after the CLARITY Act failed Senate cloture 49–50.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
TSVs must be US-based, sanctions-compliant, restrict access, and maintain public transparency. Tokenized stocks must be true digital twins (same dividend and voting rights as the underlying); purely synthetic products are excluded. Anti-fraud and anti-manipulation rules apply in full.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Why does it matter?</strong><br>
HOOD, COIN and CRCL gained 3–5%, recouping part of their post-CLARITY-fail selloff. This gives Robinhood, Coinbase, Circle, Bullish and Securitize a workable 5-year sandbox to launch on-chain equities in the US — the same product BlackRock and Kraken have been prototyping abroad.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">Who is it for?</strong><br>
Crypto-exchange traders, tokenization and real-world-asset (RWA) desks, and market-structure watchers tracking the CLARITY Act's next cloture attempt.
</p>

<h2 style="margin: 0 0 8px; font-size: 24px; font-weight: 800; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.2;">Lucid (LCID) +7.1% on Bolt deal to deploy 25,000 Level-4 robotaxis across Europe</h2>

<p style="margin: 0 0 20px; font-size: 15px; color: #8a8a85; line-height: 1.5;">Lucid signs its first ex-US robotaxi customer: European ride-hailer Bolt commits to at least 25,000 Level-4 Lucids.</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">What is it?</strong><br>
Lucid Group and Estonia-based ride-hailer Bolt announced a strategic partnership on Wednesday Sept 17 to deploy shared autonomous mobility across major European cities. Bolt committed to at least 25,000 Lucid AVs as part of a broader push to operate 100,000 AVs by 2035.
</p>

<p style="margin: 0 0 16px; font-size: 15px; color: #f5f5f0; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #f7ff00;">How does it work?</strong><br>
